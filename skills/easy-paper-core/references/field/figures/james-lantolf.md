# James P. Lantolf
**One-line identity:** The scholar who brought Vygotskian sociocultural theory (SCT) into second language studies and built it into a full research and teaching program (mediation, the ZPD, dynamic assessment, concept-based instruction).

Affiliation: Professor Emeritus and George and Jane Greer Professor in Language Acquisition and Applied Linguistics, Penn State. President of AAAL (2005). Co-editor of *Applied Linguistics* (1993-1998). Founding editor of *Language and Sociocultural Theory* (2013-2023). Won the MLA Mildenberger Prize for the 2014 pedagogical imperative book (Penn State profile).

## Gold nuggets (the 3-5 things everyone must know)
1. **Learning is social first, individual second.** Higher mental functions start between people (and with cultural tools) and are then internalized. So SCT does not treat the mind as a closed box that "processes input."
2. **Mediation is the key word.** Humans do not act on the world directly. They use tools, mostly language, to regulate the world, other people, and themselves. Language is both what you learn and the tool you learn with.
3. **The ZPD is about potential, not just current ability.** What a learner can do with help shows what is about to develop. Help must be graduated and contingent (just enough, then less).
4. **Dynamic assessment (DA) merges teaching and testing.** Instead of a static score, the assessor intervenes during the test and records how much help the learner needs. The help is the data.
5. **SCT is a theory of development and praxis.** Lantolf pushes "Vygotskian praxis": theory and practice shape each other, so research should change teaching, not only describe it.

## Core ideas in plain language (idea -> meaning -> classroom example)
- **Mediation** -> You learn by using cultural tools (language, gestures, diagrams, a teacher's questions) to regulate your thinking. -> A student reads aloud a model essay and talks through each paragraph before writing their own.
- **Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)** -> The gap between what a learner does alone and what they do with support; teaching works inside this gap. -> A tutor gives hints in steps: pause, then ask "Is there a problem here?", then point to the sentence, then give the form, until the learner needs fewer hints.
- **Regulation (object, other, self)** -> Development moves from being controlled by objects/situation, to being helped by others, to self-regulation. -> A learner first needs the teacher to flag every article error, later self-corrects while proofreading.
- **Internalization** -> Social help becomes inner speech and private thinking. -> Learners mumble grammar rules to themselves during a speaking task (private speech) before the rule becomes automatic.
- **Dynamic assessment** -> Interactive assessment that measures responsiveness to mediation. -> Instead of a cloze test with one score, the teacher records how many prompts each student needed to fix a tense error.
- **Concept-based instruction (CBI)** -> Teach the underlying concept (e.g., "aspect," "modality," "the meaning of a preposition") with visual models and materialization, not rules of thumb. -> Students use a diagram of the "container" concept behind *in/on/at* then explain choices to a peer.
- **Imitation and internalization** -> Imitation is a creative, selective act, not mindless copying. -> Learners copy a teacher's phrase, then transform it in a new context.

## Evolution of their thinking (how positions changed over time)
- **1990s:** Introduces Vygotskian ideas to L2 researchers (Lantolf & Appel, 1994, edited volume) and shows the ZPD in action through tutor-learner talk about essays (Aljaafreh & Lantolf, 1994).
- **2000s:** Moves from a "reading" of Vygotsky to a systematic L2 research program (2000 edited volume, 2006 book with Thorne). Adds activity theory, private speech, and gesture studies.
- **2004 onward:** Collaboration with Matthew Poehner makes dynamic assessment a central L2 topic (Lantolf & Poehner, 2004).
- **2010s:** Shifts to Vygotskian praxis and concept-based instruction, arguing explicitly that SCT must change pedagogy (2014 book).
- **2018-2023:** Handbook consolidation, then digital language learning and SCT (Lantolf & Xi, 2023, *TESOL Quarterly*, listed on Penn State profile).

## Main critiques and debates (who pushed back, on what grounds)
- **ZPD is used too loosely.** Kinginger (2002) argued that many US foreign language studies stretch the ZPD to mean any kind of help or grouping, losing Vygotsky's original sense. Check exact wording of that article before citing.
- **SCT versus cognitive-interactionism.** Interactionists (e.g., Long) treat feedback and recasts as input that triggers noticing. SCT treats the same moves as mediation inside a ZPD, so one event can be read two ways. SCT researchers reject the input-output computer metaphor.
- **Evidence and generalizability.** Case-study and microgenetic designs show development in detail but offer little population-level evidence. Quantitative researchers want more controlled studies.
- **Is it a theory of SLA or of learning in general?** Some argue SCT says little about specific linguistic properties; CBI is Lantolf's answer.
- **Dynamic assessment practicality.** DA is time-intensive and hard to score reliably across assessors.

## Use this when analyzing a paper (trigger -> what to say / connect / critique)
- Paper uses *scaffolding*, *ZPD*, *peer feedback*, or *collaborative dialogue* -> Ask whether the paper uses ZPD in the strict Vygotskian sense (graduated, contingent, leads to self-regulation) or just as "help." Cite Aljaafreh & Lantolf (1994) as the model.
- Paper claims "scaffolding" -> Note that scaffolding (Wood, Bruner & Ross, 1976) and ZPD are related but not identical; SCT purists object to conflating them.
- Paper on corrective feedback in tutoring -> Compare the regulatory-scale view (implicit to explicit help) with interactionist recast research.
- Paper on testing/assessment -> Propose dynamic assessment as the alternative to static tests; ask what the learner's responsiveness to help reveals.
- Paper on grammar teaching -> Link to concept-based instruction and ask whether rules of thumb or concepts are taught.
- Paper on private speech, gestures, or L1 use in L2 tasks -> Frame L1 and inner speech as mediational tools, not interference.
- Critique angle: does the paper measure *development over time* (microgenesis) or only one-time performance? SCT demands the former.

## Likely professor / exam questions (5-8, with 1-2 line model answers)
1. **What is the ZPD and why does it matter for L2 teaching?** The distance between independent and assisted performance. Teaching should target emerging abilities, using graduated help that the learner gradually no longer needs.
2. **How does SCT differ from the interaction hypothesis?** SCT sees learning as internalization of socially mediated activity and focuses on regulation and tool use; interaction hypothesis focuses on input, negotiation of meaning, and noticing inside the learner's cognition.
3. **What is mediation?** Using cultural tools (mainly language) to regulate thinking and action; development means moving from other-regulation to self-regulation.
4. **What is dynamic assessment and what are its strengths and weaknesses?** Assessment that gives mediation during testing to learn what a learner can almost do. It shows potential and informs teaching, but is time-consuming and harder to standardize.
5. **What did Aljaafreh and Lantolf (1994) find?** In tutoring sessions on ESL writing errors, the help the tutor gave moved from implicit to explicit, and learners' need for help shifted, which showed development within the ZPD.
6. **What is concept-based instruction?** Teaching the underlying meaning-based concepts of language forms with visual and verbal models, so learners can use them flexibly rather than memorize rules.
7. **Why is SCT said to be a "praxis" theory?** Because theory is tested and developed in teaching practice, and practice is shaped by theory.
