# Diane Larsen-Freeman
**One-line identity:** Applied linguist who brought chaos/complexity thinking into SLA and applied linguistics and who reframed grammar teaching as "grammaring," a skill of using forms accurately, meaningfully, and appropriately.

Affiliation: University of Michigan (Professor Emerita, as of the 2012 article she was listed there). Long associated with the School for International Training earlier in her career (recalled, not re-checked).

## Gold nuggets (the 3-5 things everyone must know)
1. **Language and its learning are complex dynamic systems.** Many interacting parts (learner, teacher, task, context, language) change over time, so outcomes are not linear and not fully predictable.
2. **Development is not a straight line.** Learners show variability, backsliding, and sudden jumps. Averages across a group can hide the real process, so look at individual trajectories over time.
3. **Emergence over rules.** Patterns in language arise from use and interaction; they are not only applied from a stored rulebook.
4. **Grammaring, not just grammar.** Grammar is a skill (a verb, not a noun). Teach form, meaning, and use together (the three-dimensional grammar framework).
5. **Learning is adaptation, and transfer is transformation.** Learners do not apply stored knowledge; they adapt and reshape what they know to new contexts (Larsen-Freeman, 2013).

## Core ideas in plain language (idea -> meaning -> classroom example)
- **Complexity / CDST** -> A system made of many interacting parts whose behavior emerges from their interaction and keeps changing. -> A class is not "a method plus students"; each lesson is shaped by who is in the room, their history, and what happened yesterday.
- **Nonlinearity and variability** -> Small causes can have big effects and development goes up and down. -> A learner's accuracy on past tense drops when they start trying longer sentences; this is a normal reorganization, not failure.
- **Attractor states and stability** -> Systems settle into temporary stable patterns that can be disrupted. -> A fossilized error persists until a new condition (feedback, communicative need) tips the system.
- **Emergence** -> New patterns arise from local interaction, not from a plan. -> A class's shared jargon develops over a term without any teacher decision.
- **Grammaring** -> Grammar as the ability to use structures accurately, meaningfully, and appropriately. -> Instead of drilling "if + past perfect," students write regrets to a friend and then notice the form, meaning, and why it fits.
- **Three-dimensional grammar (form, meaning, use)** -> Every structure has a shape, a sense, and a social function. -> Teaching the passive: how it is built, what it means, and when a writer chooses it.
- **Agency and complex systems** -> Learners shape their own development; it is not simply done to them. -> Learners choose when to speak up and which feedback to take up.

## Evolution of their thinking (how positions changed over time)
- **1970s-80s:** Early work on SLA research and teaching, including *The Grammar Book* with Celce-Murcia (1983) and the textbook *An Introduction to Second Language Acquisition Research* with Long (1991) (recalled, not re-checked).
- **1997:** The pivot article proposes that SLA be seen through chaos/complexity science (dynamic, nonlinear, open, self-organizing, adaptive).
- **2003:** *Teaching Language* turns the stance into pedagogy: grammar as a skill with form, meaning, and use.
- **2006:** An empirical case: five Chinese learners tracked over time show that complexity, fluency, and accuracy compete and fluctuate rather than rise together.
- **2008:** Book with Cameron gives the field a shared vocabulary and method guidance.
- **2012-2019:** Broadens CDST into a transdisciplinary frame, then to transfer of learning and learner agency. Term shifts from "chaos/complexity" to "complex dynamic systems."

## Main critiques and debates (who pushed back, on what grounds)
- **Metaphor versus explanation.** Critics argue CDST borrows terms (attractor, phase shift) from mathematics and physics without testable predictions in SLA. See Gregg's review criticism of the 2008 book (exact citation not verified here).
- **Methods gap.** Studying individuals over many time points is demanding. Hiver and colleagues have worked to give CDST concrete methods (Hiver & Al-Hoorie, 2020, *Research Methods for Complexity Theory in Applied Linguistics*, Multilingual Matters; recalled, not re-checked).
- **Conflict with generative and cognitive-psychological SLA.** Generativists reject the claim that language emerges mainly from use; they hold that an innate grammar constrains acquisition.
- **Relationship to Dynamic Systems Theory (de Bot).** De Bot, Lowie, and Verspoor (2007, *Bilingualism: Language and Cognition, 10*(1), 7-21) offered a related but more mathematical DST approach. Debates concern whether CDT and DST are the same thing (see the Cambridge article on their complementarity).
- **Scope.** Some say CDST is a "framework" or "meta-theory" more than a theory, so it cannot be falsified.

## Use this when analyzing a paper (trigger -> what to say / connect / critique)
- Paper reports **group averages** of development -> Raise Larsen-Freeman's point: averages can hide individual paths and variability; ask for individual trajectories.
- Paper uses **pre-test/post-test only** -> A CDST reader asks for dense time-series data and notes the design cannot show how change unfolded.
- Paper describes **fossilization, plateaus, or U-shaped learning** -> Link to attractor states and nonlinear development.
- Paper on **grammar teaching, focus on form, PPP** -> Connect to grammaring and the form-meaning-use framework; ask whether the paper teaches all three.
- Paper on **CAF (complexity, accuracy, fluency)** -> Cite the 2006 study: the measures can compete (trade-offs) and vary by learner.
- Paper on **transfer, agency, learner identity, motivation dynamics** -> Link to CDST and to Dörnyei's motivational dynamics.
- Critique angle: does the paper use CDST words (emergence, attractor) as decoration, or does the design actually track change over time?

## Likely professor / exam questions (5-8, with 1-2 line model answers)
1. **Why does Larsen-Freeman call language a complex adaptive system?** Because it is made of interacting agents and elements that adapt through use and give rise to patterns without central control.
2. **What did the 1997 article claim?** That chaos/complexity science offers a useful lens for SLA because language and learning are dynamic, nonlinear, and open to context.
3. **What is grammaring?** Using grammar structures accurately, meaningfully, and appropriately; grammar as a skill with form, meaning, and use dimensions.
4. **How does CDST change research design?** It favors longitudinal, repeated measures, individual-level data and attention to variability, over one-shot group comparisons.
5. **What did the 2006 study of five Chinese learners show?** Complexity, fluency, and accuracy developed unevenly and competed, with large individual differences, supporting a dynamic view.
6. **What is a main criticism of CDST?** That it is mostly metaphor with weak testable predictions and heavy methodological demands.
7. **What does "transfer as transformation" mean?** Learners do not apply a stored skill unchanged; they reshape it to fit each new context.
