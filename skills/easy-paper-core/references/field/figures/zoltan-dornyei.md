# Zoltan Dornyei (Zoltán Dörnyei)
**One-line identity:** The leading figure in L2 motivation research (L2 Motivational Self System, motivational dynamics, Directed Motivational Currents) and author of the most used methods texts on questionnaires and mixed methods in applied linguistics.

Born 11 March 1960, Budapest. Died 10 June 2022. PhD in Psycholinguistics (Eotvos Lorand University, 1989); University of Nottingham from 2000; Professor of Psycholinguistics from 2004 (Nottingham memorial page; Wikipedia for birth date).

## Gold nuggets (the 3-5 things everyone must know)
1. **L2 Motivational Self System (L2MSS)** has three parts: **Ideal L2 self** (the future speaker you want to be), **Ought-to L2 self** (what you feel you should be to meet expectations or avoid bad outcomes), and **L2 learning experience** (how the actual class, teacher, and peers feel).
2. **It replaced "integrative motivation."** Gardner's integrativeness (wanting to join the L2 community) did not fit contexts like EFL, where no real L2 community is nearby. Dornyei reframed it as an identification with an ideal L2 self.
3. **Motivation is dynamic, not a trait.** The process-oriented approach divides it into pre-actional, actional, and post-actional phases and later into "motivational dynamics," where motivation rises and falls with time and context.
4. **He wrote the methods books many MA students use.** *Questionnaires in Second Language Research* (2003, with Taguchi 2010 2nd ed.) and *Research Methods in Applied Linguistics* (2007) cover quantitative, qualitative, and mixed methods designs.
5. **The L2MSS has been criticized.** A meta-analysis (Al-Hoorie, 2018) found the ideal L2 self correlates strongly with intended effort but only weakly with actual achievement. See critiques below.

## Core ideas in plain language (idea -> meaning -> classroom example)
- **Ideal L2 self** -> A vivid picture of yourself as a successful L2 user motivates effort to close the gap. -> A student imagines herself presenting at an international conference in English and studies harder.
- **Ought-to L2 self** -> Pressure from parents, society, or exams. -> A student studies because family expects an IELTS score; effort is minimal and anxiety high.
- **L2 learning experience** -> Immediate influence of teacher, curriculum, peer group, and success. -> A warm class with fun tasks keeps students motivated even without a distant goal.
- **Motivational strategies** -> Teachers can deliberately create conditions for motivation. -> Clear goals, learner autonomy, positive feedback, and group cohesion (Dornyei, 2001).
- **Directed Motivational Currents (DMCs)** -> Intense, goal-oriented bursts of motivation with a clear vision and a structure that carries you along. -> A learner preparing for a wedding speech in a second language works daily with no effort feeling needed.
- **Process model** -> Motivation has phases: choosing a goal, doing the task, evaluating it. -> A teacher looks at why students started the course, why they keep going, and how they judge their progress.
- **Group dynamics** -> The class group shapes motivation. -> Rotating pairs and shared goals build cohesion.
- **Mixed methods** -> Combine numbers with interviews to compensate for each method's weaknesses. -> A questionnaire of 500 learners followed by interviews with 12 who scored extremely.

## Evolution of their thinking (how positions changed over time)
- **1990s:** Argues that Gardner's socio-educational model is too narrow for foreign-language classrooms and adds learning-situation motives (Dornyei, 1994, *MLJ*, 78(3), 273-284; recalled).
- **Late 1990s-2000s:** Process-oriented model with Ottó (1998) and large Hungarian national surveys (with Csizer) show attitudes toward English-speaking cultures shape motivation. Data pushes him to reinterpret integrativeness.
- **2005-2009:** Introduces and elaborates the L2MSS in the 2005 book and 2009 chapter. Draws on possible selves to give motivation an identity basis.
- **2010s:** Turns toward complexity and dynamics (Dornyei, MacIntyre & Henry, 2015) and DMCs. Proposes "retrodictive qualitative modelling" to study complex dynamic systems in classrooms (Dornyei, 2014, *Language Teaching*, 47(1), 80-91; recalled).
- **2017 onward:** Second doctorate in Theology (Durham, 2017) and later work on faith and language teaching, and with Mercer on classroom engagement (2020).

## Main critiques and debates (who pushed back, on what grounds)
- **Weak link to achievement.** Al-Hoorie (2018, *SSLLT*, 8(4), 721-754) pooled 32 reports (39 samples, 32,078 learners): ideal L2 self correlated about .61 with intended effort but about .20 with achievement (around .10 after publication-bias correction, non-significant).
- **Ideal L2 self items are ambiguous.** "I can imagine myself" items may measure imagination or fluency of thought, not a true desired self.
- **Ought-to L2 self is shaky.** Weak and sometimes inconsistent correlations; may work as a demotivator in some cultures. Researchers like Thompson have argued for an "anti-ought-to" self (recalled, not checked).
- **L2 learning experience overlaps with intended effort.** Item wording and weak discriminant validity.
- **Proliferation of "selves."** Al-Hoorie questions adding ever more self-constructs.
- **Intended effort is self-report.** Not observed behavior.
- **Context bias.** Most studies are on English and in Asia/Europe.

## Use this when analyzing a paper (trigger -> what to say / connect / critique)
- Paper cites **"integrative/instrumental motivation"** -> Contrast Gardner and Lambert with Dornyei's L2MSS; note that integrativeness was reinterpreted as ideal L2 self.
- Paper uses **L2MSS questionnaire** -> Check whether items come from Taguchi, Magid, & Papi (2009) style scales and whether reliability and factor structure are reported. Raise Al-Hoorie's concerns about achievement links.
- Paper claims **motivation causes achievement** -> Ask about direction: motivation may be a result of success. Most L2MSS studies are cross-sectional.
- Paper uses **Likert scales and surveys** -> Apply Dornyei (2003) guidelines: item wording, pilot, reverse items, response bias, translating into L1, length.
- Paper uses **mixed methods** -> Ask which design (explanatory sequential, exploratory sequential, convergent), why integrate, and what the mixing adds.
- Paper on **motivation over time** -> Link to process model and motivational dynamics; ask for longitudinal or time-sampled data.
- Paper on **demotivation, vision, or sustained effort** -> Link to DMCs and to vision-building interventions.

## Likely professor / exam questions (5-8, with 1-2 line model answers)
1. **What are the three components of the L2MSS?** Ideal L2 self, ought-to L2 self, and L2 learning experience.
2. **Why did Dornyei move beyond Gardner's integrativeness?** In contexts with no accessible L2 community, learners cannot "integrate," yet they still show strong motivation. An internal ideal self explains it better.
3. **Which theories underpin the L2MSS?** Markus and Nurius's possible selves and Higgins's self-discrepancy theory, plus Gardner's work on L2 motivation.
4. **What is a Directed Motivational Current?** A sustained, intense, goal-driven burst of motivation with a clear vision, a salient start, and a structure that supports progress.
5. **What are the strengths and weaknesses of questionnaires?** Efficient, scalable, standardizable data; but self-report bias, shallow answers, and sensitivity to wording.
6. **What does Al-Hoorie (2018) conclude about the L2MSS?** That it strongly predicts intended effort but only weakly predicts achievement, and that its constructs have measurement problems.
7. **What is mixed methods research?** Combining quantitative and qualitative approaches so each compensates for the other's weaknesses and the study gains breadth and depth.
