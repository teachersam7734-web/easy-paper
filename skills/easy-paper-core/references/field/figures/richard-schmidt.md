# Richard Schmidt
**One-line identity:** American applied linguist (University of Hawai'i at Manoa) who argued that conscious attention to language form, which he called noticing, is necessary for second language input to become intake.

## Gold nuggets (the 3-5 things everyone must know)
1. **Noticing Hypothesis (Schmidt, 1990).** Input does not become intake unless learners consciously notice it. Schmidt's wording of the strong claim: "subliminal language learning is impossible" (from his 1990 paper; confirm page before quoting).
2. **Noticing is not understanding.** Noticing = registering a surface feature (a form in the input). Understanding = grasping a rule or principle. Noticing is the lower level of awareness and is the minimum he claimed is needed.
3. **Origin in a diary study.** Schmidt & Frota (1986) tracked Schmidt's own learning of Portuguese in Brazil and found he learned forms he consciously noticed in input, while forms he understood but never noticed did not appear.
4. **Softened claim (1994, 2001).** Later, Schmidt weakened it: noticing is facilitative rather than strictly necessary for all learning; more noticing leads to more learning.
5. **Foundational for feedback, FFI, and form-focused research.** Recasts, enhanced input, and output studies are often judged on whether they promote noticing.

## Core ideas in plain language (idea -> what it means -> classroom example)
- **Noticing** -> Conscious registration of a form in the input. -> Learner reads a text and sees that every past event has "-ed."
- **Intake** -> The part of input that learner has noticed and processed. -> Of a whole lecture, only the phrases the listener consciously caught become intake.
- **Attention and awareness** -> Attention selects information; awareness is conscious experience. Schmidt treated attention as subjective and conscious. -> A teacher highlights "if + past" in a text so learners attend to it.
- **Noticing the gap** -> Comparing what you say to what others say (cf. Schmidt & Frota, 1986; Gass, 1988). -> Learner hears "I went" after saying "I goed" and sees the difference.
- **Levels of awareness** -> Perception (detection) < noticing (focal awareness) < understanding (rule). -> Highlighting is aimed at the middle level.
- **Frequency and salience** -> Noticing depends on frequency, perceptual salience, instruction, task demands, and processing ability. -> Third-person -s is unstressed and rarely noticed, so teach it explicitly.
- **Individual differences** -> Aptitude, working memory, and motivation affect noticing. -> Learners with high phonological memory notice more formal features.

## Evolution of their thinking (how positions changed over time)
- **1986:** Diary study with Frota (Portuguese in Brazil). Finds a link between what was noticed in input and what appeared in speech.
- **1990:** Paper on consciousness: separates levels of awareness, argues noticing is necessary for intake. Strong claim.
- **1993-1995:** Reviews attention research; stresses that attention is separate from awareness but that learning needs both in practice. Edits a Hawai'i volume on attention and awareness.
- **1994:** Hulstijn & Schmidt revision: noticing is helpful but not strictly necessary for all cases. (Revision described on Wikipedia; original citation not verified.)
- **2001:** Chapter "Attention" restates: no learning without attention; "more noticing leads to more learning."
- **2010s:** Focuses on individual differences in attention and awareness (aptitude, working memory) and notes that learners with higher ability notice more.

## Main critiques and debates (who pushed back, on what grounds)
- **Tomlin & Villa (1994):** Attention comprises alertness, orientation, and detection; detection can occur without awareness. They agreed that attention is necessary but argued awareness is not. Reference verified: *SSLA, 16*(2), 183-203.
- **Truscott (1998):** The hypothesis is vague, the cognitive psychology basis is unclear, and evidence is weak. He suggested limiting it to metalinguistic knowledge.
- **Robinson (1995):** Attention, memory, and the noticing hypothesis. Argues noticing = detection plus rehearsal in short-term memory. Reference verified: *Language Learning, 45*(2), 283-331.
- **Methodological:** Diary studies are subjective, one learner, and cannot test the "necessary" claim. Tomlin & Villa questioned the timescale (per Wikipedia).
- **Implicit learning research (N. Ellis, 2005; Williams, 2005):** Shows some learning without awareness; challenges the strong claim. Verify these citations before use.
- **Carroll (2006)** and generative critics: input alone cannot explain acquisition; noticing does not explain developing grammar.
- **Defense:** Schmidt argued that stricter tests (e.g., verbal reports, online measures) are required, and his later claim is graded.

## Use this when analyzing a paper (trigger -> what to say / connect / critique)
- Paper uses "noticing," "attention," "consciousness-raising," or "input enhancement" -> Schmidt (1990). Ask whether noticing was measured (think-aloud, stimulated recall, eye tracking) or only assumed.
- Paper tests recasts or written corrective feedback -> argue the effect depends on noticing; connect to Long, Swain, and Ellis.
- Paper on output -> Swain's noticing function is compatible with Schmidt's noticing the gap.
- Paper on implicit learning or artificial grammar -> contrast with Schmidt's strong claim.
- Paper on individual differences -> link to aptitude and working memory (Schmidt, 2010/2012; Robinson).
- Critique angle: operationalization (how can you show noticing without making it unnatural?), reactivity of verbal reports, difference between noticing and understanding.

## Likely professor / exam questions (5-8, with 1-2 line model answers)
1. **State the Noticing Hypothesis.** Learners must consciously notice features of the input for them to become intake.
2. **Noticing vs understanding?** Noticing is registering a surface feature; understanding is grasping a rule or principle.
3. **What was Schmidt & Frota (1986)?** A diary study of Schmidt learning Portuguese, showing forms noticed in input appeared in his speech.
4. **How did the claim change?** From strong "necessary" in 1990 to "facilitative, more noticing leads to more learning" in later work.
5. **Main critique?** Truscott (1998): vague and weakly supported; Tomlin & Villa (1994): awareness is not needed, detection is.
6. **How does noticing relate to Krashen's intake?** Schmidt defines intake as what is noticed; Krashen's comprehensible input needs no noticing.
7. **Which pedagogy follows?** Input enhancement, consciousness-raising, corrective feedback, dictogloss.
8. **How can noticing be measured?** Think-aloud, stimulated recall, eye tracking, learner diaries, and uptake; each has limits.
