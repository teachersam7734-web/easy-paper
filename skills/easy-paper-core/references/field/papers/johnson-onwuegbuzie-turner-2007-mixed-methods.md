# Toward a definition of mixed methods research (Johnson, Onwuegbuzie & Turner, 2007)

**One-line message:** Experts disagree on what "mixed methods" means, so the authors asked leaders in the field, analysed 19 definitions, and proposed a composite definition that treats mixed methods as a third research paradigm next to qualitative and quantitative research.

## Gold nuggets (the 3-5 things everyone must know)
1. **It is a definition paper built on an empirical mini-survey.** The authors emailed leading mixed methods methodologists (36 contacted: 31 from Tashakkori's Bridges web list plus 5 contributors to a special issue) and received **19 definitions**. Two of the 19 came from pairs of methodologists, so the unit of analysis is the definition, not the person (21 individuals contributed).
2. **The 19 definitions differ on five dimensions:** what is mixed, when/where mixing happens, breadth of mixing, why researchers mix, and orientation (bottom-up vs top-down).
3. **Their composite (general) definition** (see Key constructs): a researcher or team combines elements of qualitative and quantitative approaches for the broad purposes of **breadth and depth of understanding and corroboration**.
4. **Three paradigms, three positions.** The paper frames quantitative, qualitative, and mixed methods as the **three major research paradigms**. It also places any mixed study on a continuum with three positions: **equal status, QUAL-dominant, QUAN-dominant**. Be precise: "three paradigms" and "three positions" are two different triads.
5. **Purpose words to remember:** breadth (7 of the 19 definitions) and corroboration (5) were the most common reasons for mixing. Do not confuse "corroboration" (confirming findings) with "complementarity" (adding different angles), a later taxonomy (Greene, Caracelli & Graham, 1989).

## The problem it answered (context in that year)
- By the 2000s mixed methods had a handbook (Tashakkori & Teddlie, 2003), a journal (the Journal of Mixed Methods Research launched in 2007, this was in its early issues), and textbooks (Creswell & Plano Clark, 2007).
- But there was no agreed definition. People used "mixed methods", "multimethod", "mixed model", "triangulation" loosely and sometimes interchangeably.
- Background: the **paradigm wars** of the 1980s-90s, where purists argued qualitative (constructivist) and quantitative (positivist) research rest on incompatible assumptions (the "incompatibility thesis"). Mixed methods grew as a "third way", often tied to pragmatism.
- Without a definition, reviewers, funders and students could not tell what counts as mixed methods (e.g., is a survey with one open question "mixed"?).

## The argument / design in plain language
**Design (empirical part):**
- Sampling frame: 31 methodologists on Tashakkori's Bridges web list, plus 5 other leaders (36 contacted by email).
- Output: 19 definitions (two co-authored by pairs). Authors then did a content analysis to find "criteria of demarcation".
- Notable names attached to definitions in the discussion include Creswell, Tashakkori, Teddlie, Bazeley, Hunter, Newman, Mertens, plus Johnson and Onwuegbuzie themselves (the full table with all 19 names is not reproduced here; check Table 1 in the paper).

**Five dimensions on which the 19 definitions varied:**
| Dimension | What the analysis found |
|---|---|
| What is mixed | In 15 of 19 definitions it is qualitative and quantitative research. One definition (Hunter's) also allowed mixing within a paradigm. |
| When/where mixing occurs | 3 definitions name data collection; 2 name data collection and analysis; at least 4 say mixing can occur across all stages. |
| Breadth | Creswell: must involve both qualitative and quantitative data collection. Bazeley, Tashakkori and Teddlie: mixing at all stages. Johnson and Onwuegbuzie: also mixing worldviews and language. |
| Why (purpose) | Breadth (7) and corroboration (5) most common; a few cite meeting research aims or social justice. |
| Orientation | Newman: "bottom-up" (research question drives method). Mertens: "top-down" (emancipatory goals drive method). Authors say real studies fall on a continuum between them. |

**The three positions on the qual-quant continuum (paraphrase):**
- **Equal status (pure mixed):** the mixed researcher's "home" position, both strands matter equally.
- **QUAL-dominant (QUAL+quan):** a qualitative stance with quantitative data added.
- **QUAN-dominant (QUAN+qual):** a quantitative stance with qualitative data added.

**The three paradigms (their framing):** quantitative, qualitative, mixed methods research. Mixed methods is "the third methodological or research paradigm".

## Key constructs introduced (term | plain gloss | canonical definition w/ citation if verified)
| Term | Plain gloss | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| Mixed methods research (general definition) | Combining qual and quant elements on purpose to see more and check more | "Mixed methods research is the type of research in which a researcher or team of researchers combines elements of qualitative and quantitative research approaches (e.g., use of qualitative and quantitative viewpoints, data collection, analysis, inference techniques) for the broad purposes of breadth and depth of understanding and corroboration." (Johnson, Onwuegbuzie & Turner, 2007, p. 123; first and last clauses confirmed against secondary quotations in this session, the parenthetical list and page number come from the authors' widely cited wording, verify against the article before quoting) |
| Mixed methods research (tentative fuller definition, in the conclusion) | A paradigm, not just a technique | Opens: "Mixed methods research is an intellectual and practical synthesis based on qualitative and quantitative research; it is the third methodological or research paradigm (along with qualitative and quantitative research)." The rest, from recall and unverified in this session, adds: it partners with pragmatism (left, right, or middle), follows a "logic of mixed methods research" (induction, deduction, abduction), combines qual and quant viewpoints, data collection, analysis and inference techniques to answer the research questions, and is attentive to local and broader sociopolitical realities, resources and needs. Verify before quoting. |
| Corroboration | Using one method to confirm another | One of the two most common purposes (with breadth) |
| Breadth and depth | Wider coverage (quant) plus richer understanding (qual) | Authors' summary purpose of mixing |
| Incompatibility thesis | The claim that qual and quant paradigms cannot be combined | Target of the "paradigm wars"; mixed methods rejects it (Howe, 1988 is the classic reply, citation not verified here) |
| Pragmatism | Choose methods by what works for the question | The philosophy the authors associate with mixed methods |
| Bottom-up vs top-down | Question-driven vs goal-driven design | Newman (bottom-up), Mertens (top-down) |

## Why it matters / impact (what it changed, citation influence)
- One of the most cited papers in the mixed methods literature; the standard reference when a thesis needs "a definition of mixed methods".
- Legitimised mixed methods as a distinct paradigm and gave a quotable, concise definition (general definition) that avoids the technique-only view.
- Fed into textbook definitions and design typologies (Creswell & Plano Clark; Teddlie & Tashakkori, 2009; Johnson & Christensen).
- In Applied Linguistics, it is the usual citation behind mixed methods designs combining test scores or surveys with interviews, diaries, or classroom observation (see Dornyei, 2007, Research Methods in Applied Linguistics, for the field-specific treatment; not re-verified here).

## Known limitations and critiques (who, what)
- **Small, elite sample:** 19 definitions from methodologists chosen by reputation (a convenience/purposive frame), not a representative survey of practising researchers.
- **Consensus is thin:** the 19 definitions differ on what is mixed, when, and why; the composite is the authors' synthesis, not an agreed standard.
- **Breadth of the "paradigm" claim:** critics argue mixed methods is a design or approach and that "third paradigm" overstates its epistemological unity (compare Greene and Hall on dialectic stances; Denscombe on pragmatism; exact citations not verified here).
- **Pragmatism dependence:** tying mixed methods to pragmatism is contested; some argue mixing is possible under critical realism or dialectical stances.
- **Quality and integration gap:** a definition says what mixed methods is, not how to integrate strands well. Later work (Fetters, Curry & Creswell, 2013 on integration; O'Cathain, Murphy & Nicholl, 2008 on quality) fills this (citations from memory, unverified here).
- **"Corroboration" risk:** if a mixed study only confirms, it may underuse the qualitative strand.

## What came after (follow-up studies, revisions, meta-analyses)
- Tashakkori & Teddlie (2010) *SAGE Handbook of Mixed Methods in Social & Behavioral Research* (2nd ed.).
- Creswell & Plano Clark (2011, 2018) *Designing and Conducting Mixed Methods Research* with core designs: convergent, explanatory sequential, exploratory sequential.
- Johnson & Christensen (various editions) *Educational Research* extends the three-position idea into dominant/equal status notation (QUAL+quan, QUAN+qual, QUAL+QUAN).
- Journal of Mixed Methods Research editorials on definitions and integration.
- Mixed methods use in L2 research has been reviewed in Hashemi & Babaii (2013) in *Language Learning* (citation from memory, unverified here).

## Use this when analyzing a paper (trigger -> connection / critique angle)
- Paper says "mixed methods" -> check which of the five dimensions the authors satisfy (what mixed, when, why, breadth). Is there real integration or just two parallel strands?
- Paper uses test/questionnaire plus interviews -> ask: which strand is dominant (equal, QUAL-dominant, QUAN-dominant)? Is that stated?
- Paper claims "triangulation" -> separate corroboration (confirming) from complementarity/expansion; this paper stresses corroboration as one purpose.
- Paper justifies mixed methods by "paradigm" -> test whether pragmatism or another stance is stated and whether the design follows it.
- Paper has one open-ended survey item and calls itself mixed -> argue it fails the "combining elements" and "breadth and depth" bar.
- Methods section lacks rationale -> use the "why" dimension to ask for a justification (breadth, corroboration, explanation, development).
- Reviewing validity -> ask about inference quality across strands (legitimation, Onwuegbuzie & Johnson, 2006, not verified here).

## Likely professor / exam questions (5-8 with short model answers)
1. **How many definitions did the authors collect, and from whom?** 19 definitions from leading mixed methods methodologists (36 contacted: 31 from the Bridges list plus 5); two definitions came from pairs, so 21 individuals.
2. **State the general (composite) definition.** A researcher or team combines elements of qualitative and quantitative approaches (viewpoints, data collection, analysis, inference techniques) for breadth and depth of understanding and corroboration.
3. **On what dimensions did the definitions differ?** What is mixed, when/where mixing occurs, breadth of mixing, why (purpose), and bottom-up vs top-down orientation.
4. **What are the three paradigms and the three positions?** Paradigms: quantitative, qualitative, mixed methods. Positions on the continuum: equal status, QUAL-dominant, QUAN-dominant.
5. **Why do the authors call mixed methods a "third paradigm"?** To claim it is an intellectual and practical synthesis with its own logic (induction, deduction, abduction) and pragmatist philosophy, not just a toolbox.
6. **What were the most common reasons for mixing?** Breadth (7 of 19) and corroboration (5 of 19).
7. **Name one weakness of the study.** The sample of 19 reputation-chosen experts is small and not representative; the composite is the authors' synthesis and "paradigm" status remains contested.
8. **How does Newman's view differ from Mertens's?** Newman: bottom-up, the research question drives the design. Mertens: top-down, emancipatory goals drive it. The authors place real studies between them.

## Memory hooks and quick checks (for fast revision)
- **Numbers:** 36 contacted, 19 definitions, 21 people, 5 dimensions, 3 paradigms, 3 positions. Pages 112-133, JMMR 1(2).
- **Five dimensions mnemonic:** "WWBWO" = What is mixed, When mixing happens, Breadth, Why, Orientation.
- **Two triads, do not mix them up:**
  - Paradigms: QUAN, QUAL, MM (mixed methods is the third).
  - Positions: equal status, QUAL-dominant, QUAN-dominant.
- **Notation (Johnson's later usage):** capital letters = dominant strand, lowercase = secondary strand, plus sign = concurrent, arrow = sequential. Example: QUAL + quan, or QUAN -> qual. (This notation is from Morse and from Johnson and Christensen textbooks; not verified as appearing in this paper.)
- **Safe sentence for a thesis:** "Mixed methods research combines elements of qualitative and quantitative approaches for the broad purposes of breadth and depth of understanding and corroboration (Johnson, Onwuegbuzie & Turner, 2007)." Cite as paraphrase unless you check p. 123.
- **If asked "is a questionnaire with open items mixed methods?":** only if the design intentionally integrates both strands, with a stated purpose; otherwise it is a single-strand study with a minor add-on.
- **Typical purposes to name in a Methods section:** triangulation/corroboration, complementarity, development (one strand informs the other), initiation (new questions), expansion (greater scope). The last four come from Greene, Caracelli & Graham (1989), not from this paper.
