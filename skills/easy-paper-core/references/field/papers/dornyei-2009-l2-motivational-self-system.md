# The L2 Motivational Self System (Dörnyei, 2009)

**One-line message:** L2 motivation is best understood as the pull of our imagined future language self (who we want to become) plus the push of social expectations and the quality of the learning experience, replacing Gardner's idea of "integrativeness".

## Gold nuggets (the 3-5 things everyone must know)
1. **Three components:** **Ideal L2 Self** (the L2-speaking person I would like to become), **Ought-to L2 Self** (who I feel I should be to meet others' expectations or avoid negative outcomes), and **L2 Learning Experience** (motivation from the immediate learning environment: teacher, class, success).
2. **Core mechanism:** people want to reduce the gap between their current self and their future ideal (or ought) self. A vivid, believable ideal L2 self motivates sustained effort.
3. **It reinterprets integrativeness:** large studies found "integrativeness" correlated with the ideal L2 self. Dörnyei argues that identification is with an inner, imagined L2 self, not with an outside community (this fits global English, where there is no single target group).
4. **Built on psychology:** Markus & Nurius's (1986) **possible selves** and Higgins's (1987) **self-discrepancy theory**, plus Gardner's socio-educational model and earlier L2 motivation work.
5. **Empirical backing:** Hungarian national surveys (Dörnyei, Csizér & Németh, 2006) and the comparative Japan-China-Iran study (Taguchi, Magid & Papi, 2009, ch. 4 in the same volume) supported the system. It became the leading framework of L2 motivation in the 2010s.

## The problem it answered (context in that year)
- Gardner's **socio-educational model** (Gardner, 1985; Gardner & Lambert, 1959, 1972) put **integrative motivation** (wanting to join the L2 community) at the centre. It dominated for 30 years.
- By the 2000s, globalisation and English as a lingua franca made "the L2 community" unclear: whom do learners want to integrate with?
- The **cognitive-situated** period (1990s) had added classroom factors (Crookes & Schmidt, 1991; Dörnyei, 1994), and the **process-oriented** period (Dörnyei & Ottó, 1998) added time. The field was fragmented.
- Dörnyei's Hungarian longitudinal surveys (1993, 1999, 2004; more than 13,000 learners) showed integrativeness behaved like an internal self-concept. In Csizér & Dörnyei (2005, Modern Language Journal) integrativeness was linked to the ideal L2 self (citation from memory).
- Psychology had developed self-concept research. Dörnyei turned to it (ref. 2005, *The Psychology of the Language Learner*, where he first set out the framework).

## The argument / design in plain language
**Nature of the paper:** a theoretical and integrative chapter (pp. 9-42), the second chapter in the edited volume. It presents the model, reviews earlier L2 motivation research, and summarises empirical support. The volume's other chapters test the model: Taguchi, Magid & Papi (ch. 4, Japan, China, Iran), Csizér & Kormos (ch. 5, Hungary), Ryan (ch. 6, Japan), Yashima (ch. 7, international posture), Al-Shehri (ch. 8, imagery); the baby-and-bathwater critique by MacIntyre, MacKinnon & Clément is ch. 3 (TOC confirmed, author attributions from memory).

**The argument in steps (paraphrase):**
1. Motivation is the key to L2 learning; a good theory must explain why people invest effort over years.
2. Gardner's integrativeness cannot easily explain motivation in contexts without contact with L2 speakers.
3. In psychology, **possible selves** (Markus & Nurius, 1986) are imagined future selves acting as incentives; **self-discrepancy** (Higgins, 1987) says the gap between actual, ideal and ought selves drives behaviour. Higgins's promotion vs prevention focus also maps onto ideal vs ought selves.
4. So L2 motivation = ideal L2 self + ought-to L2 self + L2 learning experience.
5. Evidence: studies in Hungary, Japan, Iran, China and elsewhere show the ideal L2 self strongly predicts intended effort; the ought-to self is weaker and context-dependent.
6. Practical: teachers can build a vision (generate an ideal self, strengthen it, make it elaborate and plausible, link to action, keep it alive, counterbalance with feared self).

**Taguchi, Magid & Papi (2009)** (cited as the major comparative test in the volume): English learners in Japan (about 1,534), China (about 1,328), Iran (about 2,029), roughly 4,900 in total, analysed with structural equation modelling (sample sizes from a secondary table; check original).

## Key constructs introduced (term | plain gloss | canonical definition w/ citation if verified)
| Term | Plain gloss | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| L2 Motivational Self System (L2MSS) | Three-part model of L2 motivation | Dörnyei (2009): ideal L2 self, ought-to L2 self, L2 learning experience (paraphrase). |
| Ideal L2 Self | The L2-speaking self I hope to become | The L2-specific facet of one's ideal self; if the person we want to become speaks an L2, this is a strong motivator (Dörnyei, 2009, around p. 29; paraphrase). |
| Ought-to L2 Self | Who I think I should be to please others or avoid bad outcomes | Attributes one believes one ought to possess to meet expectations and avoid negative outcomes (Dörnyei, 2009; paraphrase). |
| L2 Learning Experience | Motivation from the class and learning situation | Situated "executive" motives linked to the immediate environment (teacher, curriculum, peers, success) (Dörnyei, 2009; paraphrase). |
| Possible selves | Imagined future selves (hoped for, expected, feared) | Markus & Nurius (1986). |
| Self-discrepancy | Gap between actual and ideal/ought self | Higgins (1987). |
| Promotion vs prevention | Aiming at gains vs avoiding losses | Higgins (1998); Dörnyei split instrumentality into promotional and preventional types. |
| Integrativeness | Wanting to identify with the L2 community | Gardner (1985); reinterpreted as the ideal L2 self. |
| Motivated behaviour / intended effort | Willingness to invest effort | Commonly measured outcome of L2MSS surveys. |
| Vision | Vivid mental image of the ideal future self | Dörnyei & Kubanyiova (2014) develop this into the "vision" approach. |

## Why it matters / impact (what it changed, citation influence)
- Became the dominant framework in L2 motivation research after 2009; thousands of studies (surveys, structural equation models) in Asia, Europe and the Middle East.
- Moved the field from a social-psychological focus on attitudes toward groups to a self-concept and identity focus.
- Linked motivation to **imagery and vision**, **future self-guides**, and **identity**, leading to classroom interventions (vision-building, Hadfield & Dörnyei, 2013, *Motivating Learning*; Dörnyei & Kubanyiova, 2014, *Motivating Learners, Motivating Teachers*).
- Opened bridges to **L2 identity** research (Norton) and **complex dynamic systems** (Dörnyei, MacIntyre & Henry, 2015).
- Together with Ushioda's person-in-context view, it spurred the "dynamic turn" in motivation research.

## Known limitations and critiques (who, what)
- **Conceptual vagueness:** ideal and ought-to selves overlap with other constructs (attitudes, goals, instrumental motives); the ought-to self shows inconsistent results across contexts (e.g., Kim, 2009; Papi, 2010 on anxiety linked to ought-to self; citations from memory).
- **Measurement:** questionnaires use Likert items about hypothetical futures; reliance on intended effort rather than actual behaviour; cross-sectional design.
- **Cultural bias and generalisability:** mostly English as a foreign language in East Asia and Europe; ought-to self may reflect collectivist norms.
- **Neglect of the learning experience:** the third component has been least developed and measured; much research reports only two components.
- **Meta-analytic evidence:** Al-Hoorie (2018), "The L2 motivational self system: A meta-analysis", *Studies in Second Language Learning and Teaching*, 8(4), 721-754, finds ideal L2 self and learning experience correlate with intended effort, ought-to weaker, but links to actual achievement are modest (details from memory, verify before quoting).
- **Criticism of integrative reinterpretation:** MacIntyre, MacKinnon & Clément (2009, ch. 3 in the volume) warned against throwing out "the baby with the bathwater", that is, losing valuable parts of the social-psychological tradition.
- **Static vs dynamic:** the system describes constructs, but not how they change over time (addressed by later dynamic work).
- **Self-guides can demotivate:** a distant, vague or unrealistic ideal self may not motivate (Dörnyei notes conditions: plausibility, elaboration, balance with feared self).

## Use this when analyzing a paper (trigger -> connection / critique angle)
- Paper studies "motivation" of EFL learners -> check whether it applies L2MSS; identify ideal L2, ought-to L2, learning experience scales.
- Paper uses "integrative/instrumental" -> contrast with L2MSS; mention reinterpretation of integrativeness as ideal self.
- Paper mentions "vision", "imagination", "future self" -> link to possible selves and Dörnyei & Kubanyiova.
- Paper finds ought-to self non-significant -> connect to common finding; discuss cultural context, translation of items, measurement.
- Paper uses cross-sectional survey/SEM -> critique: intended effort vs actual behaviour; no longitudinal data.
- Paper is qualitative narrative on identity -> connect to Norton's investment and Ushioda's person-in-context.
- Paper targets learners in countries without L2 community contact -> L2MSS suits this better than Gardner's model.

## Likely professor / exam questions (5-8 with short model answers)
1. **Name and explain the three components of the L2MSS.** Ideal L2 self (hoped-for L2 user), ought-to L2 self (felt obligations and fear of failure), L2 learning experience (class and situation).
2. **Which psychological theories underpin it?** Markus & Nurius's possible selves and Higgins's self-discrepancy theory (also promotion/prevention focus).
3. **Why did Dörnyei move beyond integrativeness?** In global English contexts there is no clear L2 community; surveys showed integrativeness behaved like an internal ideal self.
4. **Which component predicts intended effort most strongly?** The ideal L2 self (with the learning experience), while the ought-to self is weaker and inconsistent.
5. **What conditions make an ideal L2 self motivating?** It must be vivid, plausible, balanced with a feared self, and linked to action plans (Dörnyei & Kubanyiova, 2014).
6. **State two criticisms of the L2MSS.** Conceptual overlap and inconsistent ought-to results; reliance on self-reports of intended effort, mostly in East Asian and European EFL contexts.
7. **What did MacIntyre et al. (2009) argue?** The field should not discard the social-psychological tradition (the baby) when moving to self theory (the bathwater).
8. **How has the theory evolved?** Toward vision-based interventions and dynamic systems perspectives (Dörnyei, MacIntyre & Henry, 2015).

## Memory hooks and quick checks (for fast revision)
- **Three components mnemonic:** "I-O-E" = Ideal, Ought-to, Experience.
- **Memory image:** Ideal self is the "pull" (I want to be that person). Ought-to self is the "push" (others expect it, I fear failure). Learning experience is the "here and now" (this teacher, this class).
- **Classroom example:** a learner pictures herself as an English-speaking engineer presenting at a conference (ideal L2 self), works harder because of that picture, while her parents' pressure to pass the exam (ought-to self) adds a weaker, anxious kind of drive, and a warm class (learning experience) keeps her showing up.
- **Typical scales in a questionnaire:** ideal L2 self, ought-to L2 self, attitudes toward learning English, intended learning effort, family influence, instrumentality (promotion), instrumentality (prevention), cultural interest, attitudes toward L2 community. Look for these when a paper uses L2MSS.
- **Do not confuse:** Dörnyei's "ideal L2 self" with Gardner's "integrative orientation". Dörnyei argues the former reinterprets the latter, it does not simply rename it.
- **Date caution:** the framework first appeared in Dörnyei (2005), *The Psychology of the Language Learner* (Erlbaum); the 2009 chapter is the most-cited statement. Cite both when tracing origins.
- **Tying to other files:** a paper on motivation may also need Gardner and Lambert (1959) for lineage; a paper on identity may need Norton's investment (not in this batch).
