# Field map: Applied Linguistics, SLA and TEFL at a glance

Use this to place a paper in its lineage, choose which field files to open, and write `link` / `outside` callouts. Open at most 3 field files per paper. Anything you take from them is outside knowledge and must be labelled as such in the output.

## 3. Topic lenses without a dedicated file (verify before you cite)

When a paper's topic has no field file, the Scout or Analyst searches and verifies. Useful starting points are listed by topic. They are leads to check, not facts to repeat.
- **CALL and AI in language education:** the history of CALL (Warschauer & Healey; Bax's normalisation), technology acceptance (Davis's TAM), TPACK (Koehler & Mishra), and generative AI in L2 writing (2023 onward). Look for recent systematic reviews.
- **Academic integrity and AI:** contract cheating and plagiarism research; AI-text detector accuracy and bias against non-native writers (Liang et al., 2023, *Patterns*, is a widely cited lead to verify); publisher and COPE policies on AI authorship.
- **Language assessment:** validity as argument (Messick; Kane; Chapelle's argument-based validity for TOEFL), washback, rater reliability, automated writing evaluation.
- **Research ethics and publishing:** peer review, authorship (ICMJE), research integrity, paper mills.
- **Teacher education and cognition:** teacher beliefs (Borg), reflective practice, teacher identity.

## 4. Writing a connection callout

Use tone `link` for "this paper supports / challenges / extends theory X", and tone `outside` when the content is not in the paper. Format: theory, then the relationship (supports, challenges or extends), then one sentence on why, then one contrast. Example: "**Interaction Hypothesis (Long, 1996): extends.** An AI chatbot is an always-available interlocutor, but its feedback is directive, not negotiated, so this paper cannot speak to negotiation for meaning."
