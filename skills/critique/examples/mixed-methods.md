          Say it in 90 seconds 90 sec

       1. This is a definition paper. Johnson, Onwuegbuzie and Turner ask one question: what do we mean by mixed methods?
       2. They emailed 36 leaders, got 19 definitions back, and boiled them down to one: mix qual and quan for breadth, depth
         and corroboration.

       3. So my yardstick is simple. Whose voices did they collect, how openly did they reach the definition, and can we use it?
       4. Two strengths stand out. First, transparency. All 19 definitions are printed in Table 1, so anyone can check the coding.
       5. Second, it's usable. You get one sentence to cite, and a notation, QUAN plus qual, for your own design.

       6. Now the problems, starting with the biggest. The sample is one network, and we never learn who didn't reply.
       7. Next, the recipe is hidden. There's one coder, no matrix, and an online discussion that's mentioned once and never
         described.

       8. And integration goes missing. They call it clearly key, but the definition only says "combines".
       9. On balance, it's a quotable starting point with its evidence on the table, but it's not the last word on mixing.
       10. I'd cite the short definition, and read it alongside later work on integration, like Fetters and colleagues in 2013.

        If the examiner asks...

         What would you change?
       Report who declined, add a definition-by-theme matrix, and add "and integrates them" to the definition p. 123 .

         Isn't the sample criticism unfair? They chose leaders on purpose.

       Partly fair. Purposive sampling suits a consensus paper, and they name it openly p. 118 . But their aim is how "the
       field" defines mixed methods p. 112 , so we need to know who's in that field.
