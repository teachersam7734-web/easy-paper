# The 80/20 cut: exam-weight scoring

The goal is the 20% of the paper that would earn about 80% of the marks on a test about it, taught to someone starting from zero.

## 1. List candidates
From the dossier, list every construct, definition, framework or typology, finding or claim, number, method fact, limitation, and named person or study.

## 2. Score each one (1 to 3 per criterion)
| Criterion | 3 means | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Centrality** | Removing it breaks the paper's message | ×2 |
| **Testability** | It is an obvious exam item: definitions, typologies ("the three types"), the main finding, the main method fact, the key limitation | ×2 |
| **Confusability** | Students mix it up with something; it needs a contrast | ×1 |
| **Transferability** | It links to other course readings or SLA theory | ×1 |
Exam weight = 2C + 2T + Conf + Trans (6 to 18).

## 3. Cut line
- **16 to 18: three stars.** Page 2, top. A Hunt-Question chunk plus a visual. Appears in the mind map, the nuggets and the quiz.
- **12 to 15: two stars.** Pages 2 and 3. A chunk or a compact card.
- **9 to 11: one star.** At most one line: a mind-map leaf, a number, or a glossary-style card.
- **8 or below: cut.**
Usually 3 to 4 items get three stars, 3 to 4 get two, and the rest one.

## 4. Typical exam items in Applied Linguistics papers (prioritise these)
1. The paper's definition of its core construct (verbatim, short).
2. Any typology or list the authors name and number (the three positions, the five purposes, the four challenges).
3. The research question(s) and the design in one line.
4. The main findings, with the one or two numbers that matter.
5. The authors' main recommendation or conclusion.
6. One or two limitations, the authors' own first.
7. Who the paper builds on or argues against (one name each).

## 5. What to leave out
Literature-review lists, secondary statistics, procedural minutiae, every example after the first, and history beyond 2 or 3 milestones (unless history is the paper's centre).
