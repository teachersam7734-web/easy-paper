# Memory hooks: making the 20% stick in 2 hours

| Hook type | When to use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Acronym the paper gives you** | Named lists | Greene et al.'s five purposes, TCDIE: Triangulation, Complementarity, Development, Initiation, Expansion |
| **Your own acronym** | Unnamed lists of 3 to 6 | Four challenges: Access, Accuracy, Authorship, Assessment ("the 4 As") |
| **Contrast pair** | Confusable items | "QUAL+quan = qual leads; QUAN+qual = quan leads: the capitals are the boss" |
| **Number anchor** | Key figures | "36 asked, 19 answered, 5 themes" |
| **Image or metaphor** | Abstract constructs | "Pragmatism is a toolbox, not a church" |
| **Story** | Processes | "The detective (AI) hunts the forger (AI): who watches the detective?" |
| **Classroom example** | Applied constructs | "A teacher runs a survey (200 students), then interviews 6: QUAN → qual" |

Rules:
- One hook per must-know, and never more than one per item.
- Hooks must be accurate. A hook that distorts the idea is worse than none.
- Mark self-made hooks as such ("our hook"). Hooks taken from the paper get a page.
- Put hooks in `cards` (tone gold) on page 3, or as the last line of a chunk in `*italics*`.

## "Don't mix these up" box
2 to 3 contrasts as a `compare` block or a small `table`: item A, item B, the one-line difference, and the cue that tells them apart.
