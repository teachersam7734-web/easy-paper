          Say it out loud 60 sec

       1. Back in 2007, everyone was saying "mixed methods", but nobody agreed on what it actually meant.
       2. So Johnson, Onwuegbuzie and Turner did something simple. They asked the people leading the field.
       3. They emailed 36 methodologists, got 19 definitions back, and coded them into five themes.

       4. The themes are easy to hold: what you mix, when, how wide, why, and which way.
       5. Out of that comes one short definition: mix qual and quan for breadth, depth and corroboration.
       6. Then there's a continuum, and the capital letter is the boss: QUAL plus quan, or QUAN plus qual.

       7. And the big claim: mixed methods is a third paradigm, a research culture of its own.
       8. One honest limit. It's a consensus of one network, not a census of the whole field.

       9. So here's the question for us: when does adding a few interviews to a test really count as mixing?

        If they ask you...

         What's their definition, in one line?
       Combining elements of qualitative and quantitative research for breadth and depth of understanding and
       corroboration p. 123 . The key word is "elements": viewpoints, data collection, analysis or inference.

         Why ask the leaders instead of reviewing published studies?
       Because the job is to define a term, and leaders shape how a term is used. They call it politically important case
       sampling p. 118 . The catch: 19 definitions from one network is a narrow base.

         What does this change for researchers like us?
       It gives you a sentence to cite and a label for your design. You can write QUAN+qual and place your study on the
       continuum p. 124 .

         What's the biggest weakness?
       Integration. They call it clearly key p. 125 , but the definition only says "combines elements" p. 123 . Two strands
       that never meet could still count.

         Has anyone built on this since?
       Yes. Fetters, Curry and Creswell (2013) split integration into levels. Hampson and McKinley (2023) push back on
       pragmatism as a "paradigm of convenience".

         How does this connect to our field?
       The paper never mentions language learning, so this is my extension. Riazi and Candlin (2014) review mixed methods
       in language teaching research, and Hashemi and Babaii (2013) bring it into The Modern Language Journal.

          IF YOU BLANK
          "The core idea is one definition: mix qual and quan for breadth, depth and checking. The evidence is 19 leaders' definitions.
          The open question is how you actually integrate them."
