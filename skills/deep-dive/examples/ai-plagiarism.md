            HUNT QUESTION HQ4 pp. 4-5, 12 5 min
            What are the four ways a machine can spot AI text?

                  Watermarking hides a pattern in AI text as it is written. Training-based classifiers learn
         SHORT ANSWER
         from labelled examples. Statistical classifiers check whether a text looks as predictable as machine
         output. Retrieval-based classifiers match meaning against the AI's past outputs.
        All four approaches are binary classifiers: they sort a text into one of two boxes, AI-generated or human-
        written p. 4 . They differ in how they decide. Ibrahim takes this four-way split from Lu et al. (2023) p. 4 .
        Watermarking works at the source: while the AI writes, it nudges its word choices into a hidden pattern, a

        digital fingerprint p. 4 . Training-based classifiers learn from thousands of labelled texts. Simple ones are
        trained from scratch; fine-tuned ones start from a big pre-trained model such as BERT or RoBERTa p. 4 .
        Statistical (zero-shot) classifiers need no special training. They measure how typical of machine output a
        text's word choices are; GLTR and DetectGPT are examples pp. 5, 10-11 . Retrieval-based classifiers

        compare the meaning of a suspect text with a database of an AI's outputs, so rewording does not hide it so
        easily pp. 5, 11 .

            HUNT QUESTION HQ5 pp. 5-6, 9-12 5 min
            What is an adversarial attack, and how much does it hurt each approach?

                  An adversarial attack disguises AI text so a detector misses it, by paraphrasing,
         SHORT ANSWER
         inserting symbols, swapping synonyms or prompting for a human style. Paraphrasing, especially
         repeated paraphrasing, cuts every approach's accuracy sharply, sometimes to near zero.
        Attacks are the paper's recurring theme. Ibrahim lists four forms p. 5 : paraphrasing (rewording by hand or
        with a tool), insertion (adding random symbols, words or emojis), substitution (swapping in synonyms) and
        generative attacks (prompting or training the AI to write like a human).
