## Vocabulary index

### a promising avenue for

collocation · C1 · p. 14

a research direction that seems likely to succeed.

**Alternatives** a fruitful direction; a line of inquiry

**Use it** Eye-tracking offers a promising avenue for examining what learners notice in written feedback.

**In context** “... offer a promising avenue for improving generalizability”

### be hailed as

reporting verb · C2 · p. 5

be publicly praised as something important.

**Alternatives** be acclaimed as; be welcomed as

**Use it** Krashen's input hypothesis was once hailed as a unifying theory, yet it later drew sustained criticism.

**In context** “... scheme was hailed as a first step in utilizing watermarking to detect AI texts”

### be heightened by the fact that

structure · C1 · p. 2

a problem gets more serious because of a further fact.

**Alternatives** is compounded by; is made worse by

**Use it** The attrition problem is heightened by the fact that most longitudinal SLA studies follow few learners.

**In context** “This challenge is heightened by the fact that it is difficult to identify AI-generated content”

### capitalize on sth

phrasal verb · C1 · p. 16

use a strength or opportunity to gain an advantage.

**Alternatives** exploit; make the most of; build on

**Use it** A mixed-methods design can capitalize on both the breadth of surveys and the depth of interviews.

**In context** “a hybrid classification architecture that capitalizes on the strengths of different models”

### come at the cost of

fixed expression · C1 · p. 7

a gain is achieved only by losing or damaging something else.

**Alternatives** comes at the expense of; is achieved at the price of; involves a trade-off with

**Use it** Using a short cloze test saves time, but this efficiency comes at the cost of construct coverage.

**In context** “... reduces text quality degradation but comes at the cost of reduced detection performance”

### come under (sharp) criticism

collocation · C1 · p. 5

start to be strongly criticised by others.

**Alternatives** be heavily criticised; draw criticism

**Use it** Early error analysis came under sharp criticism for ignoring what learners avoided.

**In context** “... but it came under sharp criticism for its technical limitations”

### draw on sth

phrasal verb · C1 · p. 3

use knowledge or data as the basis for your own work.

**Alternatives** build on; make use of; be informed by

**Use it** This study draws on sociocultural theory to explain why peer feedback worked in mixed-proficiency pairs.

**In context** “the present interdisciplinary study draws on NLP research to offer ESL composition educators”

### have yet to be + past participle

structure · C1 · p. 18

has not been done so far, but it is expected or needed.

**Alternatives** has not yet been validated; remains to be validated; is still to be confirmed

**Use it** The long-term effects of data-driven learning on writing accuracy have yet to be tested in secondary schools.

**In context** “However, the actual performance of these commercial detectors has yet to be validated.”

**Usage note** Also: remains to be explored [p. 16]. Never 'has not yet to be'.

### in a similar vein

signpost · C1 · p. 8

introduces a study or point closely related to the last one.

**Alternatives** along the same lines; similarly; likewise

**Use it** In a similar vein, a second classroom study found that explicit recasts helped learners more than implicit ones.

**In context** “In a similar vein, Fröhling and Zubiaga (2021) trained a simple feature-based classifier”

### in the face of

fixed expression · C1 · p. 1

when dealing with a difficult situation or opposing force.

**Alternatives** faced with; confronted with; despite

**Use it** In the face of mixed findings, many teachers still trust their own classroom experience.

**In context** “In the face of this groundbreaking challenge, L2 educators can only rely on ... AI detectors”

### inform (a decision)

collocation · C1 · p. 16

provide knowledge that shapes or guides a decision.

**Alternatives** guide; shape; underpin

**Use it** Diagnostic test results should inform placement decisions, not replace teacher judgement.

**In context** “use AI detectors as one of many resources to inform their decisions”

### not without limitations

structure · C2 · p. 16

has some limitations; a polite way to concede weakness.

**Alternatives** has some drawbacks; is not flawless

**Use it** Self-report questionnaires are practical measures of anxiety, but they are not without limitations.

**In context** “... offer the most effective AI text detectors to date, but they are not without limitations”

### posit

reporting verb · C2 · p. 13

put forward an idea as a basis for argument.

**Alternatives** propose; hypothesise; assume

**Use it** The output hypothesis posits that producing language helps learners notice gaps.

**In context** “Zhao et al. (2023) posited that an effective detector should be distortion-free”

### predominate

academic word · C2 · p. 19

be the largest or most common element in a group.

**Alternatives** dominate; prevail; be most common

**Use it** Quantitative designs predominate in task-based research, while classroom ethnographies remain rare.

**In context** “because preprints predominate in AI text classification, many of the studies reviewed ... were not peer-reviewed”

### purportedly

hedge · C2 · p. 6

according to a claim that may not be true.

**Alternatives** allegedly; supposedly; reportedly

**Use it** The app's purportedly adaptive tasks followed the same fixed sequence for every learner.

**In context** “a theoretical account of a purportedly "undetectable" watermarking scheme”

### raise questions about

collocation · C1 · p. 12

make people doubt something or want to examine it.

**Alternatives** cast doubt on; call into question

**Use it** The small, self-selected sample raises questions about how far the findings generalise.

**In context** “raising questions about the practicality of retrieval-based classification”

### stem from the fact that

structure · C1 · p. 11

be caused by or originate in a particular fact or situation.

**Alternatives** arise from; result from; be due to

**Use it** Disagreement over task complexity stems from the fact that studies operationalise it in different ways.

**In context** “This robustness stems from the fact that paraphrasing attacks modify ... syntactic structures”

**Usage note** Never passive: not 'is stemmed from'.

### uncharted territory

idiom · C1 · p. 1

an area that nobody has studied or experienced before.

**Alternatives** unexplored ground; a new field

**Use it** AI feedback in EFL classes is still uncharted territory.

**In context** “explore the uncharted territory of AI-assisted plagiarism in L2 writing contexts”
