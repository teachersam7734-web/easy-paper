## Vocabulary index

### a stop-gap measure

idiom · C1 · PDF p. 8

a temporary arrangement used until a better or permanent one can be found.

**Alternatives** a temporary fix; an interim measure

**Use it** Hiring part-time tutors was only a stop-gap measure until the department could redesign its writing support programme.

**In context** “The policy of acknowledging AI use may be a stop-gap measure until the nature of these models can be better understood.”

**Usage note** Spelled stop-gap or stopgap; mildly critical in tone.

### anecdotally

hedge · C1 · PDF p. 10

based on personal reports or experiences rather than systematic research.

**Alternatives** in our experience; informally; judging from personal accounts

**Use it** Anecdotally, many teachers report that learners revise more when feedback is given orally, but this has not been tested systematically.

**In context** “Anecdotally, among the authors of this chapter, evidence of an AI-generated peer review for one of their papers was found on five occasions in 2023 alone.”

**Usage note** Signals weak evidence; follow it with a call for systematic data.

### at the discretion of sb

fixed expression · C1 · PDF p. 8

decided by a particular person or body, according to their own judgement.

**Alternatives** up to; left to; decided by

**Use it** Whether learners may use dictionaries during the writing test was left at the discretion of each class teacher.

**In context** “the extent to which AI usage is allowed or barred is likely to remain at the discretion of individual publishers”

**Usage note** Also "at sb's discretion". "Up to" is less formal.

### be adept at sth / doing sth

collocation · C1 · PDF p. 4

be skilled and quick at a particular task.

**Alternatives** be skilled at; be proficient in; be good at

**Use it** Advanced learners in the study were adept at paraphrasing source texts but struggled to signal their own stance.

**In context** “AI models are adept at idea generation.”

**Usage note** Preposition is "at", not "in" or "with". "Good at" is informal.

### hold promise for

collocation · C1 · PDF p. 10

seem likely to be useful or successful in a particular area in the future.

**Alternatives** show potential for; look promising for

**Use it** Corpus-based tasks hold considerable promise for teaching collocations to EAP students, although classroom evidence is still limited.

**In context** “it is clear that they hold significant promise for the future application of AI in the preliminary review process of a journal paper”

**Usage note** Use "hold" or "show" promise, not "have a promise".

### in conjunction with

fixed expression · C1 · PDF p. 4

together with something else, so that the two are used or considered at the same time.

**Alternatives** together with; alongside

**Use it** Stimulated recall interviews were used in conjunction with classroom observation to explain the teachers' feedback choices.

**In context** “the models should be used in conjunction with other information sources”

**Usage note** Always "in conjunction with", never "in conjunction to".

### judiciously

academic word · C2 · PDF p. 11

with careful, sensible judgement about when and how much to use something.

**Alternatives** carefully; sensibly; prudently; wisely

**Use it** L1 use in the EFL classroom can support comprehension when it is applied judiciously rather than as a default.

**In context** “but believe that they can use it more judiciously to make suggestions for the authors”

**Usage note** Do not confuse with "judicially" (relating to courts).

### less than satisfactory

structure · C1 · PDF p. 7

not good enough; a polite, understated way of saying something is poor.

**Alternatives** unsatisfactory; inadequate

**Use it** The pilot questionnaire yielded less than satisfactory reliability, so two items on learner anxiety were rewritten.

**In context** “These models are by no means perfect and can produce less than satisfactory outputs on occasion”

**Usage note** Hyphenate before a noun in strict style: less-than-satisfactory results.

### the extent to which

structure · C1 · PDF p. 8

how far, or how much, something is true or happens.

**Alternatives** the degree to which; how far; to what extent

**Use it** This study examines the extent to which task repetition improves the oral fluency of intermediate EFL learners.

**In context** “Ultimately, the extent to which AI usage is allowed or barred is likely to remain at the discretion of individual publishers”

**Usage note** Keep "to which"; "the extent that" is informal.

### there is some debate as to whether...

structure · C1 · PDF p. 8

Scholars disagree about whether something is true or needed; the question is still open.

**Alternatives** it remains contested whether; opinion is divided on whether; scholars disagree about whether

**Use it** There is some debate as to whether explicit corrective feedback benefits low-proficiency learners more than implicit recasts do.

**In context** “There is some debate as to whether attribution of a large language model is actually necessary if that model is simply used as a tool to enhance writing and grammar”

**Usage note** Use "as to whether", not "about that" or "as to that".

### virtually indistinguishable from

collocation · C1 · PDF p. 7

so similar to something else that the difference can hardly be seen.

**Alternatives** almost identical to; nearly impossible to tell apart from; much the same as

**Use it** By the final term, the learners' pronunciation of the target vowels was virtually indistinguishable from that of the native-speaker baseline group.

**In context** “a model like ChatGPT can rework lengthy sections of a paper to read at a level virtually indistinguishable from a professional writer or editor”

**Usage note** "Indistinguishable from", not "indistinguishable to" or "than".
