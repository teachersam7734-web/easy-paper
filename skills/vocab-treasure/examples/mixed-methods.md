# Defining Mixed Methods (Johnson et al., 2007) · Vocab Treasure

By Sajjad Irani.

> Recovered from the approved PDF, Defining Mixed Methods (Johnson et al., 2007) - Vocab Treasure.pdf. The original carries the exact layout, illustrations and wording. This reading copy preserves the visible PDF page order.

## Vocabulary index

### albeit

academic word · C2 · p. 127

although; adds a limiting point to what was just said.

**Alternatives** although; if only; though

**Use it** The intervention improved accuracy, albeit only for the structures taught explicitly.

**In context** “all three approaches are considered to be important and needed, albeit in different circumstances.”

### be contingent on

collocation · C2 · p. 130

depend on something happening or being true.

**Alternatives** depend on; hinge on; be conditional on

**Use it** Fluency gains appeared contingent on the amount of out-of-class practice.

**In context** “This is contingent, for example, on an equal probability selection method (EPSEM) being used”

### be credited with being the first to ...

structure · C1 · p. 114

be publicly recognised as the person who first did or introduced something.

**Alternatives** be acknowledged as the first to; be regarded as the pioneer of; be the first to

**Use it** Selinker is usually credited with being the first to use the term interlanguage for the learner's developing linguistic system.

**In context** “Thus, Webb et al. are credited with being the first to coin the term triangulation.”

**Usage note** Use "with" + -ing; the idea itself is "credited to" a person.

### bring sth to bear on

idiom · C2 · p. 120 (quoting Patton)

use knowledge, evidence or ideas in order to deal with a particular problem or question.

**Alternatives** apply X to; draw on X to address; use X in

**Use it** The study brings sociocultural theory to bear on why learners take up some corrections and ignore others.

**In context** “using different data sources and design elements in such a way as to bring different perspectives to bear in the inquiry”

### by extension

signpost · C2 · p. 127

applying the same logic more widely, as a further result.

**Alternatives** by implication; consequently

**Use it** Teacher beliefs shape practice and, by extension, students' learning opportunities.

**In context** “allows for the coexistence of quantitative and qualitative research approaches and, by extension, provides a philosophical foundation”

### by virtue of

fixed expression · C2 · p. 115

because of a quality or feature that something has.

**Alternatives** because of; owing to; on account of

**Use it** By virtue of its longitudinal design, the study traces writing across a full year.

**In context** “by virtue of its comprehensiveness, it may serve as the litmus test for competing theories”

### call sth into question

reporting verb · C1 · p. 117

raise doubts about whether something is true, valid or needed.

**Alternatives** question; cast doubt on; challenge

**Use it** These results call into question the claim that explicit instruction suits all learners.

**In context** “calling into question the need for the divisions or differentiation”

### come to grips with

idiom · C1 · p. 117 (quoting Schwandt)

begin to understand and deal with something difficult.

**Alternatives** grapple with; deal with; make sense of

**Use it** Novice raters need a full semester to come to grips with analytic rubrics.

**In context** “the traditional means of coming to grips with one’s identity as a researcher”

### foreshadow

reporting verb · C2 · p. 114

hint at an idea before it is fully developed or named.

**Alternatives** anticipate; prefigure; point ahead to

**Use it** Corder's work on learner errors foreshadowed the later concept of interlanguage.

**In context** “Boring (1953) foreshadowed this concept as follows”

### forthcoming

academic word · C2 · p. 112

available or provided when expected; often used in the negative.

**Alternatives** available; on offer; provided

**Use it** Funding for the replication study was promised but not forthcoming until year two.

**In context** “there might not be a perfect or essentialist definition forthcoming”

### give credence to

collocation · C2 · p. 116

make an idea or claim seem more believable.

**Alternatives** lend credence to; lend support to

**Use it** The interview data give credence to the view that anxiety limited participation.

**In context** “made statements that appear to give credence to mixed methods research”

### include, but are not limited to, ...

fixed expression · C1 · p. 130 (quoting Johnson & Onwuegbuzie, 2004)

signals that a list gives examples, not every item.

**Alternatives** include, among others,; such as

**Use it** Data sources include, but are not limited to, diaries, stimulated recall and screen recordings.

**In context** “The beliefs include, but are not limited to, ontological beliefs”

### more of an X than a Y

structure · C1 · p. 114

be closer to one category than another; corrects an inflated label.

**Alternatives** less a Y than an X; closer to an X

**Use it** Task repetition is more of a fluency technique than a teaching method.

**In context** “is more of a measurement and construct validation technique ... than it is a full research methodology”

### most, if not all, ...

structure · C1 · p. 123

most and possibly every one; a careful way to make a broad claim.

**Alternatives** nearly all; the great majority of

**Use it** Most, if not all, participants used machine translation for at least one task.

**In context** “as one considers most, if not all, research questions.”

### not nearly as X as is suggested in the literature

structure · C1 · p. 117

far less X than others claim; corrects an overstatement.

**Alternatives** much less X than assumed; far less X than claimed

**Use it** The implicit-explicit boundary is not nearly as sharp as is often suggested in the literature.

**In context** “these positions are not nearly as “logical” and as distinct as is frequently suggested in the literature.”

### something of a misnomer

fixed expression · C2 · p. 121 (quoting Sandelowski)

a name that is somewhat misleading for what it describes.

**Alternatives** a misleading label; an inaccurate name

**Use it** The "silent period" is something of a misnomer: many learners rehearse privately.

**In context** “I see mixed methods as something of a misnomer as mixing implies blending together.”

### what sets X apart from Y is that ...

structure · C1 · p. 121 (quoting Shulha)

introduces the feature that makes one thing different from the rest.

**Alternatives** What distinguishes X is; X differs in that

**Use it** What sets this study apart from earlier work is that it tracks three drafts.

**In context** “What sets the most complex forms of ... mixed method research apart from other forms of inquiry is that”
