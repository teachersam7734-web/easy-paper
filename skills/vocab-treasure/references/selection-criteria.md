# What counts as treasure

Goal: language an MA/PhD student in Applied Linguistics will **reuse in their own academic writing** (proposals, theses, articles, critical commentaries, conference talks). The question for every candidate: *would I be glad to have this ready when I write my literature review or discussion section?*

## Categories (use these exact type labels)
| Type label | What it is | Treasure examples |
|---|---|---|
| collocation | words that habitually go together | lend credence to, a growing body of research, warrant further investigation, bridge the gap |
| fixed expression | multiword unit with a stable form | in light of, by and large, with respect to, to a lesser extent, on the grounds that |
| structure | grammatical frame with slots | it remains to be seen whether..., what is at stake is..., not so much X as Y, the extent to which... |
| hedge | softens a claim | arguably, tend to, it could be argued that, appears to suggest |
| reporting verb | verb for citing others, with its pattern | contend that, posit, call into question, concede that |
| signpost | organises the argument | to this end, against this backdrop, in a similar vein, it follows that |
| phrasal verb | academic-register phrasal verb | draw on, account for, point to, give rise to |
| idiom | figurative expression acceptable in academic prose | a case in point, the jury is still out, a double-edged sword, the tip of the iceberg |
| academic word | a single C1-C2 word of high reuse value | salient, ubiquitous, conflate, tenable, nuanced, exacerbate |

## Scoring (1 to 3 each; keep 10 or more out of 12)
| Criterion | 3 means |
|---|---|
| **Reusability** | Fits many papers and sections, not only this topic |
| **Level** | Clearly C1-C2: a strong B2 writer would not produce it naturally |
| **Precision payoff** | It says something more exactly or more elegantly than the plain alternative |
| **Risk of misuse** | Learners often get its form or preposition wrong (worth learning properly) |

## The reject list (be picky)
- B2 and below: important, significant, however, show, increase, factor, aspect.
- Field jargon and constructs (mixed methods, recast, watermarking): these belong in the glossary of the other modes, not here. Exception: a technical phrase with general academic use (a growing body of evidence).
- Coinages and names (AICEF, QUAN+qual).
- Clichés that reviewers dislike (in today's world, a myriad of, plays a pivotal role, delve into).
- Errors or non-standard usage in the paper (do not canonise a typo or an awkward L2 phrasing; skip it).
- Anything already in the Treasure Chest. Check `Treasure Chest/treasure-chest.csv` if it exists.

## How many
- About 9 to 10 entries per page; 1 page per about 10 pages of article (13 pages gives 1; 20 to 24 gives 2).
- Mine 25 to 40 candidates and keep only the top scorers. Spread across types: no more than 3 of one type per page.
- A paper written in plain language may yield only 7 or 8 real treasures. That is fine. Fill the space with the quiz, never with weak entries.
