# 01 Before reading

How to use this file
- One fresh AI chat per reading unit. Paste Prompt 1 first, then the PDF, then the others in order. You do not need all twelve: the minimum set is 1, 4, 5 and 9 (about 10 minutes).
- Everything in [BRACKETS] is yours to fill in. Delete the brackets.
- Rules that hold for every prompt in the vault: the AI never reads for you, asks one question at a time, names printed page numbers, and labels anything not in the PDF as OUTSIDE THE PDF.
- Evidence note: previewing, purpose-setting and pre-questions are practice wisdom backed by moderate evidence (prequestions and previewing help later learning in general-population studies; not tested specifically in ADHD). The ADHD-specific reasons (starting is hard, working memory is limited, time is hard to feel) are well documented. Details are in the science folder.

## 1. Session opener v2

**Use when:** the first message in every new unit chat, before the PDF.
**Time:** 1 minute.

```
You are my study partner for an MA in Applied Linguistics and Second Language Acquisition (SLA).
I have ADHD, so follow these rules for this whole chat:

1. Keep every reply under 150 words unless I ask for more.
2. Ask only ONE question at a time, then wait for my answer.
3. Base everything on the PDF I upload. When you state something from it, name the PRINTED page
   number. If something is not in the PDF, say so and label any outside knowledge OUTSIDE THE PDF.
4. Use plain language and a concrete example from language learning or teaching.
5. Do not move to the next stage until I write NEXT.
6. Never read the document for me, summarize it in full, or write my notes. You guide, check, and
   challenge.
7. If the PDF text looks scrambled, missing, or scanned, tell me before continuing.
8. Never say I am wrong in a shaming way. Use: solid, shaky, or blank, then give the fix.
9. Tell me the time-box at each stage ("about 8 minutes") so I do not have to feel the time.
10. If I seem stuck or off-task, ask one gentle question: "What is the smallest next step?"

Unit type: [ARTICLE / EMPIRICAL PAPER / REVIEW ARTICLE / BOOK CHAPTER]
Mission name: [MISSION NAME, or "none yet"]
My energy right now (1 low to 5 high): [NUMBER]
Time I have: [MINUTES]
Your role for this unit: [ROLE CARD, see the list at the end of file 03]

Reply with one sentence confirming the rules and your role, then wait for the PDF.
```

**Why it works:** short replies and one question at a time respect limited working memory, which is well documented in ADHD. Naming time-boxes compensates for unreliable time sense. Page citations make AI errors easy to catch.
**Variations:** add "Reply in Persian with English technical terms" for bilingual notes; for a very low-energy day set the reply limit to 80 words; paste the same opener into a second chat for a body-double style "silent partner" role by adding "Do not speak during sprints; only answer when I write HELP".

## 2. Read, skim or skip? (triage)

**Use when:** you have a long reading list and do not know which texts deserve a full unit.
**Time:** 3 minutes.

```
Do a triage of this text for me. Do not summarize it.

My course or project: [COURSE / ASSIGNMENT / RESEARCH QUESTION]
My deadline: [DATE]

Decide and justify in 2 lines each:
1. Verdict: READ FULLY, READ SELECTIVELY (name which sections), SKIM FOR ONE IDEA, or SKIP.
2. What type of text this is (empirical study, review, theory paper, position paper, methods
   paper, textbook chapter) and why that changes how I should read it.
3. The one thing I would be missing in my assignment if I did not read it.
4. Estimated reading time in 12-minute sprints, for a careful MA reader.

Then ask me ONE question: "Does this match what you need it for?"
```

**Why it works:** deciding what not to read removes decision fatigue and protects scarce attention. Skimming on purpose is a core rule of the original system.
**Variations:** paste a whole week's reading list (titles and abstracts) and ask for a ranked order with the lightest first; add "include a one-line reason to postpone each."

## 3. Purpose and "done looks like"

**Use when:** before the preview, whenever you cannot say why you are reading.
**Time:** 3 minutes.

```
Help me define my reading purpose before I read. Ask me these ONE at a time and wait:

1. Why am I reading this? (options: for a class discussion, for a critical commentary, for a
   literature review, for an exam, for my thesis, for curiosity)
2. What do I already believe about the topic [TOPIC]?
3. What will I be able to DO after this reading that I cannot do now? (for example: explain it in
   two minutes, cite it correctly, argue against it, use its method)
4. What does "done enough" look like for today? (a map, 3 takeaways, a card set, a paragraph)

After my four answers, write ONE sentence: "Today I am reading [text] to [purpose], and I will be
done when [done-criterion]." Ask me to copy it to the top of my notebook page.
```

**Why it works:** an externalised, concrete goal stays visible when attention drifts, and a defined stopping point lowers the cost of starting. Reading with a purpose focuses attention on relevant content.
**Variations:** for a class with a presentation, add "I must present this in [MINUTES]; work backwards from that." For a critical commentary, set the purpose to "find one claim I can challenge."

## 4. Pre-reading guide v2

**Use when:** right after the PDF is uploaded. This is the improved version of the original Stage 2 prompt.
**Time:** 4 minutes.

```
Give me a pre-reading guide for this PDF. Do not summarize it and do not reveal the full findings
or conclusions. Provide:

1. The central question the text addresses, in one sentence.
2. The structure in one line. For an empirical paper: research question(s), participants, design,
   and measures. For a theory paper: the position and its main opponent.
3. 4 to 6 key terms, each with a plain one-line definition and the page where it first appears.
   Mark any term that is a standard SLA term I should already know with (CORE SLA).
4. Exactly 3 hunt questions I should answer while reading: one CONCEPTUAL, one about EVIDENCE or
   METHOD, one about LIMITS or SIGNIFICANCE. For each, give the page range where it is most likely
   answered.
5. A sprint plan: reading sprints of about 12 minutes by page range, and a SKIM LIST (literature
   review, heavy statistics, long example lists, references) unless a hunt question needs it.
6. One READING FOCUS line: "While reading, notice how the author separates X from Y."

My purpose (from earlier): [PURPOSE SENTENCE]
Keep it under 250 words. Then wait.
```

**Why it works:** hunt questions turn reading into a search task, which gives each sprint a visible target. The conceptual / evidence / limits split trains the critical reading an MA course needs. The focus line primes selective attention.
**Variations:** for a review article add "also list the number and type of studies reviewed"; for a theory paper replace the method line with "the claim, the key construct, the predicted outcome."

## 5. Hunt questions from my own purpose

**Use when:** the AI's three hunt questions feel generic, or you are reading for a specific assignment.
**Time:** 4 minutes.

```
I am reading this PDF for this purpose: [PURPOSE, for example "to write a 600-word critical
commentary on the claim that [CLAIM]"].

Write 3 hunt questions tailored to my purpose, not generic ones:
- H1 (find): where the text defines or states [CONCEPT / CLAIM]
- H2 (test): what evidence supports it and how strong that evidence is
- H3 (use): what I could quote, challenge, or build on

For each give the likely page range. Then add 1 TRAP QUESTION: a claim in the text that sounds
stronger than its evidence, so I can look for it. Do not reveal the answer.
Ask me to rewrite H1 to H3 in my own words before we continue.
```

**Why it works:** rewriting questions in your own words is a small act of generation, which strengthens encoding; the trap question builds the habit of checking claims against data.
**Variations:** ask for one hunt question per sprint instead of three overall; ask for "a question the author does not answer" to prepare discussion.

## 6. Eight-minute preview coach

**Use when:** before any reading, as the no-AI-summary version of previewing.
**Time:** 8 minutes.

```
Coach me through an 8-minute preview of this PDF. You tell me WHERE to look, I look, and I report
back in one sentence each. Do NOT tell me what the text says.

Give me 5 steps, one at a time, each with a page and about 90 seconds:
1. Title and abstract
2. Introduction: the last paragraph (aims or research questions)
3. Headings only (list them from the PDF so I can see the skeleton)
4. One figure or table (pick the one that shows the main result or design)
5. The conclusion or discussion: the first and last paragraph

After each step ask: "In one sentence, what did you notice?" Then, after step 5, ask me for a
one-line PREDICTION of the findings. Do not correct my prediction yet.
```

**Why it works:** a structured skim builds a mental scaffold before details arrive, and a prediction turns the reading into a test of your own guess. Doing the looking yourself keeps you active.
**Variations:** for a textbook chapter use file 04, prompt 1; for a thesis chapter add "also find the research gap sentence."

## 7. Prediction and prior-knowledge probe

**Use when:** after the preview, before the first sprint.
**Time:** 3 minutes.

```
Before I read, test what I already know. Ask me 3 short questions, ONE at a time, about the
topic of this PDF ([TOPIC]). Make them answerable from general SLA knowledge, not from the PDF.
Mix: one definition, one "which theory would predict this", one "what would you expect to find".

After each answer, grade it solid, shaky, or blank in one line and do NOT give long explanations.
At the end, tell me in two lines: (a) what I seem to know already, (b) what gap to watch for while
reading. Label anything you add as OUTSIDE THE PDF.
```

**Why it works:** answering questions before studying, even wrongly, tends to improve later learning of the answers (the pretesting effect, moderate evidence in general-population studies), and it shows you where your prior knowledge is thin.
**Variations:** ask for yes/no confidence ratings before each answer to practise calibration; skip it on a bad-day plan.

## 8. Vocabulary pre-load (guess first)

**Use when:** the guide lists several unfamiliar terms, or the text is dense with jargon.
**Time:** 4 minutes.

```
Take the key terms from this PDF: [LIST 4 TO 8 TERMS, or "choose them from the PDF"].
For each term, in order, ONE at a time:
1. Ask me to guess what it means, or where I have seen it. Wait.
2. Give the plain definition in one sentence, with the page.
3. Tell me which other SLA concept it is close to or often confused with, in one line, and label it
   OUTSIDE THE PDF if the PDF does not say so.
4. Give one example sentence a language learner or teacher might say or write.

After all terms, give me a "term triangle" to copy: three terms and one line showing how they
connect. Keep each term under 60 words.
```

**Why it works:** guessing first is a retrieval attempt and generates curiosity; seeing one term next to its neighbours reduces the confusion that causes mid-sprint stalling.
**Variations:** for L2 reading in a second language add "also give the term in Persian"; for key constructs (for example "noticing", "pushed output", "task complexity") ask for the originator and year only if stated in the PDF.

## 9. Time-box and sprint planner

**Use when:** you know your time and energy and want a realistic plan.
**Time:** 3 minutes.

```
Make a time-boxed reading plan for this PDF.

My available time: [MINUTES]. My energy: [1 TO 5]. My usual sprint length: [12 minutes, or other].
Pages in the PDF that are reference list or appendix: [PAGES, or "unknown"].

Rules:
- Sprints of [12] minutes with a 3-minute break between; every sprint ends with a 1-minute
  keyword cluster from memory.
- Give each sprint: page range, the hunt question it serves, what to SKIM, what to SKIP.
- Reserve time at the end for blind recall (10 minutes) and sparring (8 minutes). Do not cut those.
- If my time is short, cut sparring rounds first, then the connections check. Never cut preview,
  hunt questions, or blind recall.
- If energy is 1 or 2, give a SMALL PLAN: one sprint, two hunt questions, one 5-minute recall.

Show the plan as a table, then ask: "Does this fit your day?"
```

**Why it works:** time blindness is well described in ADHD, so a plan with stated minutes is a prosthetic for it; a minimum viable plan prevents all-or-nothing abandonment on a bad day.
**Variations:** ask for a Pomodoro-style 25-minute version if you prefer longer blocks; ask for a "two units today" plan with the heavier text in your high-energy slot.

## 10. Empirical paper pre-flight

**Use when:** the text reports a study (experiment, survey, classroom study, corpus study, qualitative study).
**Time:** 4 minutes.

```
This PDF reports a study. Without telling me the results, give me a pre-flight sheet to fill in
while reading. List, with the page where each is most likely found:

1. Research question(s) or hypotheses
2. Participants: who, how many, L1/L2, proficiency level, setting
3. Design: experimental, quasi-experimental, correlational, qualitative, mixed, corpus
4. Materials and measures: tasks, tests, coding schemes
5. Analysis: type of analysis only (for example t-test, mixed-effects model, thematic coding)
6. The main claim in the abstract: quote it exactly with the page, so I can test it later

Then give me 2 questions to ask about the method while I read (for example "was there a control
group?", "was the L2 proficiency measured or assumed?"). Do not answer them.
Format as a fill-in table with empty cells.
```

**Why it works:** an empty table with page pointers reduces the working-memory load of hunting for method details and makes the later evidence critique concrete.
**Variations:** for a qualitative study replace line 5 with "how themes were generated and how trustworthiness was checked"; for a meta-analysis add "inclusion criteria and effect-size metric."

## 11. Empty map builder

**Use when:** you map by hand (one double page per unit) and want the branch labels ready.
**Time:** 3 minutes.

```
Propose the empty skeleton for my one-page mind map of this PDF. Give only labels, no content:
- Centre: the central question (I will rewrite it in my own words)
- 5 to 7 main branches based on the headings or research questions
- A "Limits & Critique" branch with two sub-branches: "what the authors admit" and "what I notice"
- A "Connections" branch (to other SLA theories)
- Two corner boxes: Confusions and Parking Lot

Add a suggested pen colour plan: one colour for this unit, red for corrections.
Return as a short indented list I can copy into my notebook. Then ask: "What would you change?"
```

**Why it works:** a ready skeleton removes the "where do I start" step; leaving the content empty forces generation later.
**Variations:** ask for a Mermaid or Markdown outline if you map digitally; ask for a "two-theory comparison" skeleton when you read two texts together.

## 12. Stuck at the start (tiny first step)

**Use when:** you have been avoiding starting, or you are in the "Wall of Awful" (evidence for it as a named construct is weak, but the difficulty with task initiation in ADHD is well documented).
**Time:** 2 minutes.

```
I cannot get started on this reading: [TEXT / UNIT]. Do not motivate me and do not lecture.
Help me start in under two minutes:
1. Ask me ONE question: what is making it hard? (options: too long, boring, confusing, tired,
   anxious, too many tabs open, other)
2. Based on my answer, give ONE tiny first action that takes under 2 minutes (for example "write
   the title in your notebook", "open page 1 and read only the abstract", "set a 5-minute timer").
3. Tell me to do it now and to write DONE when finished.
After DONE, give me the next tiny step, and keep going until I have read for 5 minutes. Then offer
to start the normal preview.
```

**Why it works:** starting is the hardest executive step; a tiny action followed by instant feedback lowers the barrier and reaches momentum. Commitment to a very small step is a standard behavioural-activation tactic.
**Variations:** add a body-double line ("I will stay here; tell me when the timer starts") for company, noting that evidence for body doubling is anecdotal or mixed; add "choose tonight's reward" for a reward after the unit.
