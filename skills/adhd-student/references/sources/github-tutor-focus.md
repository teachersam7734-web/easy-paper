## 3. Patterns found (what the sources agree on)

1. **One question at a time, then wait.** Sources 1, 6, 7 all insist on a single question per turn. For ADHD this limits working-memory load. Every tutor prompt in the vault repeats it.
2. **Ask before explaining.** Prior knowledge, level and goal first (1, 6). Our prompts add a fourth item: time and energy available today.
3. **Hints before answers; try twice before reveal.** Tutor gives a hint ladder (nudge, narrower question, partial example, answer) (1, 6, 8).
4. **Exit condition = student output.** The session ends when the student explains in own words, links an example, or applies the concept to a new case (1). We made this explicit as a "done test".
5. **Never ask "do you understand?"** (1). Replace with a check task.
6. **Teach-the-novice role play** (1) is the common "protege" pattern. We offer chatty and skeptical learner modes.
7. **Mentor feedback without writing the work** (1, 7): strengths first, one main fix, student states the next action. This is the safest pattern for academic writing and for avoiding AI plagiarism.
8. **Prompt anatomy**: identity, limits, behaviour, format, and a short length (7). Under 200 words works best for pasting at the top of a chat.
9. **Task breakdown tools converge on about five micro-steps with time estimates and a two-minute starter** (9, 11). We copy the pattern but cap steps at the student's energy, and add a "first physical action" line.
10. **Focus tools offer shorter sprints and visible time** (9, 10). Our planning prompts default to 10 to 25 minutes with a visible timer, matching the original system's 12-minute sprint.
11. **Awesome-lists for ADHD mix evidence with marketing.** Row 10 contains unreferenced neuroscience claims. Vault files cite none of them.
12. **No source solves the "AI does the thinking for me" risk by itself.** Only the prompt (hints, not answers) and the student's habit (write first, then ask) do. File 08 and 09 build this into the workflow.

## 4. Design decisions that follow

- Every prompt in 05 to 09 includes: a one-question-at-a-time rule where it is interactive, a stop condition, a "tell me when you are unsure or guessing" clause (AI can confabulate, source 2), and a request to cite page numbers or quote the pasted text only.
- Prompts that ask the AI to summarise source text say "use only the text I paste; if it is not there, say so".
- Prompts for writing never ask the AI to produce the final prose for assessed work; they produce questions, outlines, checks and feedback, or, where drafting help is allowed, labelled scaffolds the student rewrites. Students must follow their own university's AI policy.
- ADHD adaptations: short replies, numbered options, visible timers, a parking lot, a "stuck ladder" (see 06), and no shame language.
- Not medical advice. The vault never discusses medication or dosing.
