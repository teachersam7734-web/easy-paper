# Design principles from the science

Each principle has: the evidence behind it (author-year, full references in `../sources/academic-science.md`, mechanism detail in `neuroscience-and-psychology.md`), an evidence label (strong, moderate, emerging, practice wisdom), a **Page rule** (what the document does) and a **Prompt rule** (what an AI guide or study-buddy prompt does).

Important framing: these principles apply to anyone who wants friendlier, structured, time-efficient study. They are not a diagnosis or treatment. Where evidence is general-learning science and not ADHD-specific, the label says so.

## Group A: Reduce load on working memory

### 1. Every chunk opens with its answer
- **Evidence:** Working memory deficits are among the largest in ADHD (Martinussen et al., 2005; Pievsky & McGrath, 2018); cognitive load theory (Sweller, 1988). Strong (deficit), moderate (design inference).
- **Page rule:** Each chunk starts with a one-sentence "Point" line, then the support. No buried conclusions.
- **Prompt rule:** "Start every section with a one-sentence takeaway, then the explanation."

### 2. Small chunks with one job each
- **Evidence:** Segmenting reduces load (Mayer & Moreno, 2003); executive and attention limits (Barkley, 1997). Moderate.
- **Page rule:** One idea cluster per chunk, roughly a screen or a 5 to 10 minute read. A chunk title says what the chunk does ("Define the gap").
- **Prompt rule:** "Break the material into chunks of no more than [N] ideas; label each chunk with its purpose."

### 3. Define at first use, repeat the short form later
- **Evidence:** Limited working memory for unfamiliar terms (Kasper et al., 2012; Sweller, 1988). Moderate.
- **Page rule:** Inline mini-definition when a term first matters; a glossary strip at the chunk end for terms used later.
- **Prompt rule:** "Define each technical term in one plain sentence the first time it appears; do not assume I remember it."

### 4. A running recap, not a memory test
- **Evidence:** Working memory limits; training does not transfer (Rapport et al., 2013; Cortese et al., 2015). Strong (no transfer).
- **Page rule:** A "So far" box after each chunk with 2 to 3 bullets. Never require the reader to hold earlier chunks in mind.
- **Prompt rule:** "After each section give a 'so far' recap of at most three bullets before continuing."

### 5. Short sentences, plain structure, no nested conditions
- **Evidence:** Load from complex syntax follows working memory and cognitive load reasoning (Sweller, 1988). Moderate (inference).
- **Page rule:** Aim for short sentences, bullets for lists, numbered steps for sequences, tables for comparisons.
- **Prompt rule:** "Use short sentences. Replace long conditionals with a bulleted checklist."

### 6. Keep technical accuracy, simplify the packaging
- **Evidence:** Graduate readers need precision; the load problem is structural, not conceptual (Gioia et al., 2026 found complexity-sensitive comprehension gaps). Moderate.
- **Page rule:** Keep the real terms and citations; add a plain gloss next to them. Never replace the term.
- **Prompt rule:** "Keep technical terms exact. Add a one-line plain explanation after each; do not rewrite the claim."

## Group B: Start, sustain and re-enter

### 7. A tiny first step for activation
- **Evidence:** Brown's activation cluster (Brown, 2013); initiation difficulty in student outcomes (DuPaul et al., 2021). Practice wisdom, supported by executive-function data.
- **Page rule:** Page 1 ends with "Your first 2 minutes": one action that needs no decisions.
- **Prompt rule:** "Give me a first step that takes under two minutes and needs no preparation."

### 8. If-then launch plans
- **Evidence:** Implementation intentions improve goal attainment (Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006) and helped response inhibition in children with ADHD (Gawrilow & Gollwitzer, 2008). Moderate.
- **Page rule:** A fill-in "If [time/place], then I [first action]" line at the top of each study session.
- **Prompt rule:** "Ask me for a time and place, then write one if-then plan for starting."

### 9. Every chunk is re-enterable
- **Evidence:** Lapses and reaction time variability (Kofler et al., 2013); mind wandering (Lanier et al., 2019; Bozhilova et al., 2018). Strong (variability), moderate (mind wandering).
- **Page rule:** A running header names the document, the chunk number and a "you are here" line. Each chunk has a two-line "If you lost the thread" recap.
- **Prompt rule:** "If I say 'I'm lost', restate where we are in three lines and give the next step."

### 10. A parking lot for intruding thoughts
- **Evidence:** Spontaneous mind wandering is more frequent in ADHD (Lanier et al., 2019); DMN lapses (Sonuga-Barke & Castellanos, 2007). Emerging.
- **Page rule:** A small "Parking lot" box on each study page for stray tasks or ideas.
- **Prompt rule:** "When I mention an unrelated task, add it to a parking-lot list and bring me back to the chunk."

### 11. Active tasks over passive reading
- **Evidence:** Retrieval beats restudy (Rowland, 2014; Adesope et al., 2017); idle reading time allows drift (Bozhilova et al., 2018). Strong (general), emerging (ADHD).
- **Page rule:** Every chunk includes an action: predict, sort, fill, explain, draw, answer.
- **Prompt rule:** "Never just summarize. After each part, ask me one question I must answer before moving on."

### 12. Place-keeping cues
- **Evidence:** Inattentive symptoms (loses place, easily distracted) are central (American Psychiatric Association, 2022). Strong (symptom), practice wisdom (design).
- **Page rule:** Checkbox per chunk, bold key sentence per chunk, a visible "stopped here" marker.
- **Prompt rule:** "Number every step so I can say 'continue from step 4'."

## Group C: Time and pacing

### 13. Show time, do not rely on feeling it
- **Evidence:** Timing deficits across the lifespan (Marx et al., 2022; Metcalfe et al., 2023; Noreika et al., 2013); adult everyday evidence mixed (Mette, 2023). Strong (lab), emerging (daily life).
- **Page rule:** Every chunk shows an estimated minutes badge and a total at the top.
- **Prompt rule:** "Give each step a time estimate and a total. Pad estimates by about 25 percent."

### 14. Fixed, short work blocks with a visible stop
- **Evidence:** Delay aversion and sustained effort (Sonuga-Barke, 2003); Brown's effort cluster (Brown, 2013). Moderate (theory), practice wisdom (block length).
- **Page rule:** Blocks of 10 to 25 minutes, then a stop line: "Stop here is fine."
- **Prompt rule:** "Work in [N]-minute blocks. At the end of each block, ask whether I want to continue or stop."

### 15. Calibrate: estimate, then compare
- **Evidence:** Time estimation variability (Marx et al., 2022). Practice wisdom for the calibration habit.
- **Page rule:** A "Guess / Actual" pair on the session log.
- **Prompt rule:** "Ask me to guess how long the next task will take, then compare with how long it took."

### 16. A visible finish line
- **Evidence:** Delay aversion (Sonuga-Barke, 2003; Luman et al., 2005). Moderate.
- **Page rule:** Open with "This document has N chunks, about M minutes. You do not have to finish today."
- **Prompt rule:** "Always tell me how many steps remain and about how long they will take."

## Group D: Reward, interest and emotion

### 17. Quick, frequent, honest wins
- **Evidence:** Reinforcement sensitivity (Luman et al., 2005); reduced reward anticipation signals (Plichta & Scheres, 2014); dopamine transfer theory (Tripp & Wickens, 2008). Moderate.
- **Page rule:** Checkbox on each chunk, a "you can now say..." line that names a gain in plain terms.
- **Prompt rule:** "End each step by naming one thing I now understand or can do."

### 18. Link the material to the reader's goal
- **Evidence:** Motivation to study predicted next-year GPA (Franklin et al., 2018); interest-driven engagement (Luman et al., 2005). Moderate (motivation), practice wisdom (interest hooks).
- **Page rule:** Open with a question that ties the reading to the reader's thesis, assignment or class.
- **Prompt rule:** "Before we start, ask me why this reading matters for my project, and use my answer to frame the session."

### 19. Vary the task type
- **Evidence:** Novelty and reinforcement effects (Luman et al., 2005); no direct adult trials. Emerging, practice wisdom.
- **Page rule:** Alternate read, predict, sort, explain, check every few minutes.
- **Prompt rule:** "Switch the activity type every [N] minutes: summary, question, sketch, explain-back."

### 20. Calm, non-judgmental tone with repair moves
- **Evidence:** Emotional dysregulation is common and relevant to outcomes (Shaw et al., 2014; Stevens et al., 2021; DuPaul et al., 2021). Moderate.
- **Page rule:** A "Stuck?" box: "Too big? Too boring? Too tired? Pick one: shrink it, swap it, rest."
- **Prompt rule:** "If I say I am stuck or frustrated, respond kindly, name one smaller step, and never scold."

### 21. No streak-shaming; allow gaps
- **Evidence:** Discouragement after setbacks and persistence gaps in students (DuPaul et al., 2021; Stevens et al., 2021). Moderate (outcomes), practice wisdom (design).
- **Page rule:** No broken-streak counters. A "Welcome back" restart box on every document.
- **Prompt rule:** "If I come back after a gap, restart from the last finished chunk with no lecture."

### 22. Treat hyperfocus as an experience, give it an exit
- **Evidence:** Hyperfocus is reported but poorly defined (Hupfeld et al., 2019; Ashinoff & Abu-Akel, 2021). Emerging.
- **Page rule:** A "Set an alarm for a break and a meal" line in the session header.
- **Prompt rule:** "If I say I'm deep in flow, remind me of my planned stop time when it arrives."

## Group E: Learning strategies

### 23. Retrieval practice at the end of every chunk
- **Evidence:** Testing beats restudy (Rowland, 2014; Adesope et al., 2017); high-utility technique (Dunlosky et al., 2013). Strong (general), emerging (ADHD-specific, none located).
- **Page rule:** 2 to 3 self-check questions per chunk, with answers hidden at the bottom.
- **Prompt rule:** "Ask me 3 questions about what I just read; wait for my answers before telling me the correct ones."

### 24. Spaced review with a built-in schedule
- **Evidence:** Spacing improves retention (Cepeda et al., 2006; Dunlosky et al., 2013). Strong (general).
- **Page rule:** A review table: "Tomorrow, in 3 days, in 1 week", with 3 questions each.
- **Prompt rule:** "Create a review plan with questions for 1 day, 3 days and 7 days later."

### 25. Offer movement as an optional break
- **Evidence:** Exercise benefits in children with ADHD (Cerrillo-Urbina et al., 2015); inner restlessness in adults (American Psychiatric Association, 2022). Moderate (children), emerging (adults).
- **Page rule:** A "Move for 2 minutes (optional)" suggestion at block ends.
- **Prompt rule:** "At each break suggest one 2-minute movement option; accept 'no thanks'."

### 26. External structure beats willpower
- **Evidence:** Executive functions as self-regulation across time (Barkley, 1997; Barkley, 2012); executive functioning predicted student outcomes (DuPaul et al., 2021); coaching gains are small and early (Parker & Boutelle, 2009; Prevatt & Yelland, 2015). Moderate.
- **Page rule:** Plan, checkpoint, schedule and review sections are part of every output, not an appendix.
- **Prompt rule:** "Make the plan, the checkpoint and the review part of your answer every time."

## Group F: Boundaries and honesty

### 27. No diagnosing, no medical advice
- **Evidence:** Diagnosis is a clinical process (American Psychiatric Association, 2022); treatment evidence is context dependent (Cortese et al., 2018). Strong.
- **Page rule:** A short footer: "A study aid, not a diagnosis or treatment."
- **Prompt rule:** "If asked about diagnosis or medication, suggest a qualified clinician and stay neutral; never give dosing."

### 28. State evidence strength; do not oversell
- **Evidence:** Many popular ADHD claims outrun the data (see "Limits" in neuroscience-and-psychology.md). Strong.
- **Page rule:** When a page cites science, label it strong, moderate, emerging or practice wisdom.
- **Prompt rule:** "When you give a reason for a technique, say how strong the evidence is. Do not invent studies."
